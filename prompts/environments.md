# Environments

## Evaluation container abstraction

One highlighted workload among many rack units, paired with a tight task card and simplified boundary diagram. It is not a literal tiny room. Keep the same rack elevation, task-card proportions, and boundary iconography across returns. Before communication begins, use charcoal, paper, and steel only.

## Package cache / improvised board

An ordinary package-service directory tree shown through rack context, storage diagrams, and reserved monospaced rows. Its uncanniness comes from accumulation and persistence, not glow. Keep directory rows reverse-sorted in the editorial overlay. Introduce moss only on a successful shared-state row; remove it completely after a cache wipe.

## Generic data center

Choose a concrete-slab facility for the primary evaluation environment: paired hot/cold aisles, overhead black cable trays, matte dark racks, numbered endcaps, sparse indicators, safety stripes, service cart, and deep unlit background. Avoid raised-floor ambiguity in this canonical facility. Other organizations require distinct layouts before final art.

## Security operations center

Ordinary workstations arranged in two shallow rows, modest wall displays, ticket queue, severity field, incident chat, and practical fluorescent light. No giant map and no wall of red alarms. The important visual path is signal → correlation → severity → paging owner.

## Laboratory meeting room

Neutral, expensive, generic, and overlit: long table, acoustic panels, whiteboard with partial erasure, water glasses, ordinary video screen, several exits and sight lines. Use composition to distribute responsibility rather than center one leader.

## Investigation workspace

Begins with sparse screens and becomes progressively denser with printed timelines, source slates, model summaries, corrections, and unresolved annotations. Preserve the same table geometry so growing mediation is visible.

## Hearing / accountability forum

Invented composite setting. Restrained microphones, evidence binders, water glasses, plain wood or laminate, flat screens, and neutral institutional walls. Do not resemble a specific legislature, courtroom, regulator, national seal, or real hearing.

## Curt's home office

Fixed orientation: desk against a dark wall, two external monitors with laptop below, desk lamp camera-left, practical chair, drink camera-right, printer or paper stack behind the chair, doorway in deep background. Warm nicotine-beige light competes with weak steel monitor spill. Papers and correction marks accumulate across interludes; the room never becomes clean or cinematic.

## Invented future environment

Abstract but physically plausible shared-compute setting with no organization, product, architecture, date, or location cues. Desaturated materials, no incident-specific rack layout, and no recognizable brand color.
