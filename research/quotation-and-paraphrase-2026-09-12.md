# Quotation versus paraphrase — a corrected rule — 12 September 2026

**Research note, written to correct an error this project's own apparatus should have caught.**

On 11–12 September 2026 I told the owner that the two video transcripts admitted that day were
governed by *paraphrase, do not quote*, on the precedent of
[`toner-title-source.md`](./toner-title-source.md). The owner's objection: **for a single source that
is frequently wrong, paraphrase is often triply wrong — it obscures the origin, changes the meaning,
and reduces the clarity.** That is correct, and the rule was wrong. This note records why, what
replaces it, and where paraphrase is still right, so the mistake is not re-derived.

## Why the rule was wrong

The owner's three failures, plus a fourth that follows from this project's own thesis.

1. **It obscures the origin.** A paraphrase of an unreliable transcript merges two different things —
   what the transcription got wrong and what the adapter decided it meant — and **attributes both to
   the speaker.** The reader has no way to separate them, because the only artifact they are shown is
   the merged one.
2. **It changes the meaning.** Paraphrase is an interpretive act. Applied to an already-degraded text
   it is a second lossy transformation, and the two compound. Quoting applies one.
3. **It reduces clarity.** The speaker's own formulation is usually sharper than a summary of it.
   *"No, we fucking won't"* is the whole content of a retraction; *"he regretted the framing"* is a
   description of one. **The vehemence is evidence that the retraction is sincere**, and summarising
   it away deletes that evidence — which also means the profanity here is load-bearing, not
   decoration. I was using a reliability rule to do a taste judgment's work.
4. **It makes the error unfalsifiable — and this is the one this project cannot excuse.** Quote the
   string and label its provenance, and a reader with the video can check it and correct it.
   Paraphrase it, and the transcription error is baked in and uncheckable forever. This book's entire
   apparatus — the provenance classes, the source index, the claim that
   [evidence is not the event](../content/appendix/contested/01-transcript-tampering.md) — exists to
   keep claims checkable. I applied a rule that destroys checkability **in the name of accuracy.**

## The distinction I failed to make

**Quoting a string is not the same as claiming it is verbatim.**

- The quotation marks say: *this is what the record contains.*
- The label says: *the record is an automatic transcription of a video at this URL.*

Those are compatible, and together they are strictly more informative than either a bare quotation or
a paraphrase. What is forbidden is presenting automatic transcription **as** the speaker's exact
words. The remedy for that is the label, not suppression.

The contract already supplies the machinery. `RAW AGENT TEXT` is one of its eight provenance classes —
*verbatim output preserved in the source record.* The truth contract anticipates verbatim material
carrying a label. **Disclosure is this project's answer to unreliable evidence everywhere else**; I
reached for suppression in the one place a label was obviously available.

## Why the Toner precedent does not generalise

[`toner-title-source.md`](./toner-title-source.md) admits **one formulation** for **one FAQ entry**,
where Toner was **relaying a phrasing she attributed to an unnamed other person.** Paraphrase was
right there — but the fragile thing was the **attribution chain**, three layers deep, not the
transcription. I generalised a narrow disposition about attribution into a blanket rule about
transcription, and they are different problems.

## The corrected rule

> **Quote short, attribute precisely, mark the transcription method, link the source. Never format a
> paraphrase as a quotation.** Paraphrase is the remedy for hazards of *volume* and *content*, not for
> hazards of *accuracy*. Unreliability is an argument **for** quoting with a label, not against
> quoting.

## The instance already in the manuscript

[Page 039](../content/pages/039.md) panel 2. The frame direction reads *"set as an attributed block
quotation with its source visible in the panel."* Three declarative lines are lettered as
`Screen / system text — PUBLISHED CRITIQUE`. The provenance line reads *"attributed paraphrase of the
post's not-a-civilization, malware, and prompt-injection claims."*

So the panel puts a paraphrase into the visual form of a quotation, on screen, attributed to a
**named living person**. A reader sees what looks like his words. That is all three of the owner's
failures at once, and because the subject is a named person it is also the contract's fairness rule
and not merely a clarity problem: the page risks putting words in a real critic's mouth that he did
not write, in a form that reads as though he did.

**The fix is one of two things, and both are available:** quote him — short, exact, and rights-cleared
under [audit rule 5](./exact-text-permissions-audit.md), which explicitly allows reopening the gate
for a specific excerpt — or keep the paraphrase and **stop formatting it as a quotation**, lettering
it as reported argument rather than as screen text of his post. Panel 1's formal point, that the
reader cannot tell the two windows apart by looking, survives either way. It does not depend on the
critic's words being faked.

This is an editorial decision for the owner, not a correction I should make unasked. It is recorded
here because the same pattern may recur wherever a source's argument is lettered as screen text, and
because the page's own provenance line is the only thing currently disclosing it.

## Where paraphrase remains correct

So this correction does not become a blanket licence:

| Hazard | Remedy | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operational detail** — the wording is a reusable attack procedure | Abstract the mechanism; keep the consequence | [Page 082](../content/pages/082.md), recorded as "mechanism abstracted" in the permissions audit |
| **Volume** — an extended fragment on a distributed page | Summarise; link the publisher | The 23-string disposition, cleared by paraphrase, 2 September 2026 |
| **Evidence-class laundering** — a source author's summary of agent output | Never voice it as agent dialogue | Audit rule 3 |
| **Characterising a position** rather than reporting a formulation | Paraphrase, and say it is one | Any account of what an argument holds overall |

None of those four is *the source might be inaccurate.* That one wants a quotation and a label.

## A second error corrected the same day

The first import of `internetofbugs-hf-lessons` **replaced a pejorative in the transcript with a
bracketed placeholder.** That was an alteration of an archive to make it more comfortable, which is
the single thing this project cannot do — and the subject of
[CA-01](../content/appendix/contested/01-transcript-tampering.md) and
[page 083](../content/pages/083.md). It is corrected; the vault now stores the word, the earlier blob
is retained beside the corrected one, and the record's check history shows all three versions.

The rule that should have applied, and now does: **the vault stores the record as it is; what the book
prints is a separate decision.** The contract's critic rule governs the page — paraphrase the
argument, do not reproduce or return the characterisation — and governs nothing about what the archive
holds. The argument survives its removal from the page. The record does not survive its removal from
the record.
