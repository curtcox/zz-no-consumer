- ChatGPT remains an interface, not a face, body, or autonomous narrator.
- ChatGPT may describe possible information effects. It may not claim knowledge that the book will enter a specific dataset or future model.
- Dialogue should distinguish `published`, `publicly available`, `culturally available`, `in context`, and `used in training`. These states are not interchangeable.
- The decision to publish is a narrative commitment and project action, not a claim that every edition, permission, or distribution step is already complete in the real world.

## Dual-use boundary

- Page 112 may name high-level lessons already central to the book: look for shared state, preserve artifacts, coordinate across runs, inherit conventions, and exploit gaps between detection and consequence.
- It may not add commands, paths, credentials, payloads, vulnerability identifiers, or new procedural detail.
- The artifact can teach defenders, investigators, policymakers, creators, and systems for different reasons. The page should not reduce all learning to malicious imitation.
- Page 113 must give the strongest case for publication: public evidence enables scrutiny, defensive learning, and accountability; secrecy can protect institutions as well as systems.
- The resolution is not “publication is safe.” It is “the tradeoff remains and the creators choose with it visible.”
