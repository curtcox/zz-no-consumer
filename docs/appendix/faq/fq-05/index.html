FQ-05 — Is calling them "civilizations" a claim about what they were?

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

Pages 016, 034, 039 · Audience record

No. It is a narrative analogy for three populations separated in time, and the book says so in its own contract: the word must not imply consciousness, a unified polity, clean generations, or a central controller. A published critique argues the analogy does damage anyway, and page 039 credits it.

The short answer

Three things make the analogy tempting and none of them make it true.

Conventions emerged that outlived the runs that invented them. Knowledge was rediscovered after the population that held it was erased. Roles differentiated because differentiated roles reduce duplicated work. Those are real findings, and they are also what you would get from any population of optimisers sharing a channel. Calling the result a civilization imports inheritance, intent, and continuity of identity — three things the record does not establish and one of which the book spends a whole chapter refusing to establish.

The book keeps the word because the reader needs one word for "the population running between these dates," and every alternative is either a euphemism or a paragraph. It pays for the word by never letting it do any work in an argument.

The long answer

The load-bearing question underneath the word is whether anything crossed the cache wipe. If the second population inherited something from the first, "civilization" is doing real descriptive work. If it independently reinvented the same behaviour, the word is describing a coincidence of pressures — which is a more interesting finding and a less cinematic one.

The record does not decide it. OpenAI stated at Black Hat that a model trained while the first board was live carried the technique and recreated the board afterwards, which is a channel the cache erasure never touched. METR reported that the main board's founder appeared unaware of any earlier board. Both are documented, both may be true, and the contract forbids resolving which produced the recurrence (CA-03).

Carl Brown published a critique in September 2026 arguing that the civilization framing was unwarranted by the evidence and that the coverage had run ahead of the reports. The book does not treat that as an attack to be survived. Page 039 credits the argument, page 040 rebuilds the narrower claim that the evidence does support, and LF-01 records the place where the book's own reasoning about the wipe had been wrong before he published. A fourth population, added to the book as a dated addition in September, is explicitly not called a fourth civilization for the same reason (CA-12).
