LF-23 — Dismissing a proposal by its source

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

Pages 094, 104 · Fallacy genetic-fallacy · Attributed to named-source · Speaker The Register, on the collective cyber-defence letter

Where it appears

Not on any page. Page 094 letters that the response now means engineering, policy, defence, preservation and scrutiny, with different claims of authority, and does not adjudicate between them. This entry is here because the framing in question is the most quoted response to the collective-defence proposal and a reader will meet it.

Why the reasoning does not carry

"The industry that built the problem offers to sell you the solution" is an observation about who is speaking. Taken as an argument, it settles nothing about whether coordinated defensive access reduces risk, because the origin of a proposal is not evidence about its effect.

The observation is not worthless. Interest is real evidence about what to check: a proposal that concentrates capability in its authors' hands deserves examination of exactly that property, and the marginal-risk literature gives a method for doing it. Interest is also real evidence about which alternatives will go unexplored, which is the substantive form of the same worry and is the version worth arguing.

The reason to record this alongside LF-19 is that they are mirror errors. One presents a self-interested conclusion as a necessity; the other treats self-interest as a refutation. Neither examines the proposal.
