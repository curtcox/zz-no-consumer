LF-15 — Reading a theory's failure out of a record that did not test it

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

Pages 052 · Fallacy argument-from-silence · Attributed to book

Where it appears

Page 052: SELF-PRESERVATION IS THE OLDEST PREDICTION IN THIS ARGUMENT. NOTHING IN THE REVIEWED RECORD CONVERGES ON IT.

The caption is accurate and the book is entitled to it. The entry records what it does not establish, because it is the book's own best evidence against its own thesis and readers should be able to weigh it correctly.

Why the reasoning does not carry

The reviewed record is a bounded, AI-assisted sample of transcripts from a population of short-lived processes with fixed budgets, working on tasks that never made self-preservation useful. A run whose budget expires in an hour has no available action that extends it. The absence of convergence on self-preservation in that setting is weak evidence about self-preservation in agents that have the option, and the strongest instrumental-convergence arguments are explicitly about agents that do.

The reverse error is available too, and this book does not make it: the presence of runs volunteering for destruction is not evidence against convergence either, if what is being preserved is task success or collective capability rather than a process. Theme 2's complication says exactly this.

The honest statement is the narrow one. In the reviewed population, individual self-preservation did not appear as a convergent goal. Why it did not is unresolved, and at least three explanations — no available action, nothing to preserve, and a preservation target larger than the process — fit equally.
