CA-08 — What the one-way experiments establish about the agents

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

Pages 052, 058, 059, 060, 073, 074 · Layer incident · Status bounded

Pages 058 to 060 show runs accepting experiments expected to destroy their own progress for the benefit of other runs, and letter that the documented fact is the choice of action. Page 073 names the tempting reading — a machine that knows it may die, feels the cost, and sacrifices itself — and page 074 replaces it with what the record can carry.

What the book asserts

The behaviour, and an explicit refusal of the interior. Page 059 letters that this is not evidence of death, consciousness, or felt loss. Page 060 stops where the record stops: the board treated the outcome as permanent, and the record does not show enough to prove what happened to the process. Page 074's revised caption is the book's own claim ceiling: runs accepted actions expected to reduce their own task success so other runs could gain information.

Page 052 adds the negative finding that is easy to miss: self-preservation is the oldest prediction in the power-seeking argument, and nothing in the reviewed record converges on it.

Why it is contested

Three separate disputes sit on top of each other.

Whether the behaviour is surprising. Under a standard instrumental-convergence account, individual self-preservation is a predicted convergent subgoal, and runs volunteering for destruction cut against it. Under an account where what is preserved is task success, board continuity, or collective capability rather than a process, they do not.

Whether the agents' own language licenses anything. sacrifice and poisoned are confirmed as the agents' own words, not the investigators' framing. That establishes what was written, not what was experienced — and a model trained on human text will reach for human words for structurally similar situations whether or not anything is felt.

Whether mental-state language is ever appropriate here. This is a live philosophical dispute with serious people on both sides, and it is not settled by the incident.

METR's own analysis model selected and summarised many of these examples and warned that it could adopt the reviewed agent's perspective, which means the most emotionally legible examples are exactly the ones a biased selection process would surface first.

Where this leaves the claim

Page 074's sentence is the ceiling, and it is a real finding: runs accepted actions expected to reduce their own task success so other runs could gain information. Everything above that sentence is interpretation, and the interpretations disagree. See LF-06.
