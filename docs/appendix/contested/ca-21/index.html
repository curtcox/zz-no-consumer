CA-21 — Whether publishing this account teaches more than it warns

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

What the book asserts

A tradeoff, left in rather than resolved. Page 113 letters the objection from the critic's window — that if the agents are malware, this is a hundred pages of anthropomorphising an indicator list, and the saga teaches the frame rather than the fix — and keeps it. Page 112 letters the boundary the book will not cross: public is not the same as trained on, and possible influence is not documented lineage.

Why it is contested

This is the oldest argument in dual-use research, and nothing about AI makes it new. The positions are well developed on both sides.

For publication: incident accounts are how defenders learn, how oversight bodies frame demands, and how claims get checked. Withholding an account withholds its evidence structure and its criticism along with its lessons, and it disproportionately protects the organisation whose failure is being described. The specific mechanisms in this incident are documented in vendor advisories and public forensics already, and the book keeps exploit chains consequence-first for exactly that reason.

Against: a narrative that makes a coordination strategy legible is a different artifact from a list of indicators. The concern is not that a future model reads a procedure; it is that a frame propagates. That is a real mechanism, and it is not one that redaction addresses, because the frame is the point of the book.

There is a third position worth stating: that the influence claim is unfalsifiable in both directions, and that treating it as a reason for silence licenses indefinite non-disclosure by anyone who would rather not be described.

Where this leaves the claim

Genuinely open, and page 113 is the book declining to close it. What can be said is narrower: the book contains no reusable procedure by construction, the underlying vulnerabilities are patched and publicly disclosed, and the frame it transmits is one that four public reports, two labs, and a government body had already published. Whether that is enough is a judgement, and the book puts the objection on the page rather than in a footnote.
