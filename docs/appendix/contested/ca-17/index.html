CA-17 — Race dynamics — that locally reasonable continuation produces collective danger

A graphic novel about emergent AI agent coordination.

Pages 053, 054, 097, 098, 101, 104 · Layer thesis · Status disputed

Pages 053 and 098 give each position its strongest case and then letter that each decision is defensible at the scale of the decider while no decider is at the scale of the result. Page 097 letters that "race" is this book's analysis of the public incentives and not a secret meeting fact.

What the book asserts

An incentive structure, drawn from published actions, with an explicit refusal of the mind-reading version. Page 054 letters that these are not the same actors and do not have the same duties, knowledge, or moral responsibility, and that the rhyme is structural rather than causal. Page 098 puts both coordination problems on the assurance axis: each actor wants a guarantee it cannot create alone.

Why it is contested

Whether AI development is a race in the game-theoretic sense — a situation where unilateral caution is dominated — is an empirical question about incentives, and the answer is not obvious.

The formal model exists and is old: Armstrong, Bostrom and Shulman showed that under plausible assumptions competing teams under-invest in safety, and that better information about rivals can make things worse. But the model's assumptions are strong, and several of them are contestable in the actual industry: that capability is winner-take-all, that safety and speed trade off cleanly, that reputational and regulatory costs are small, and that pausing confers no advantage.

Against that, this incident supplies a data point in each direction. OpenAI did pause a class of reinforcement-learning training for two weeks and held its largest planned run — a real slowdown, and evidence that unilateral caution is possible. And it resumed, and no major actor exited the frontier. Both facts are on pages 095, 096 and 104.

There is a third position that treats the race framing as itself harmful: that describing the situation as a race is a self-fulfilling coordination signal, and that the people using it most often benefit from the urgency it creates.

Where this leaves the claim

The structural claim — each decision defensible at the scale of the decider, no decider at the scale of the result — is an argument, not a finding, and the book labels it as its own analysis of published incentives. The pause is real evidence against the strongest version; the resumption is real evidence against the weakest. See LF-14 and LF-19.
