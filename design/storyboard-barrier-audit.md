# Barrier and informational-background audit

Reviewed the full storyboard inventory against canonical scene frames on 12 September
2026. The strongest recurring ambiguity was a barred gate standing for several
unrelated constraints: unavailable network reach, privilege, manual review, reduced
request policy, a scoped hold, and irreversible action. Thin dividers likewise mixed
access boundaries with evidence limits and chronology cuts.

The revision adds 15 reusable components and applies them to 58 existing scenes.
Generic gates and dividers remain useful where the script names only a broad boundary
or an editorial comparison. No new scene, narrative event or canonical wording is
introduced. Earlier component and artwork versions remain intact.

## Composition decisions

The preserved-storage enclosure surrounds only the archived side of the rebuild;
live traffic and the clean cache stay separate. The source-reservoir background holds
board dump and transcript inputs in independent trays, and their foreground folder
and transcript shapes now differ. Parallel calendar rails align the two histories
without a bridge or extra event marks. Review disposition trays keep resumed,
change-required and paused outcomes legible as separate operational states.

Color remains scene-controlled. None of these new symbols asserts that a defense is
universally effective. A network wall is an abstract restriction, not architectural
evidence. The administrator opening stays inside the attributed later-account
sequence. The one-way control remains unpressed even where the recorded decision is
to try it. The unknown outcome remains unknown.

Visual review covered the new palette and thirteen representative lettered panels.
It prompted wider thin dividers, narrower pause bars for shallow trays, and distinct
foreground forms for the two evidence reservoirs. Pattern detail never supplies
counts, source text, credentials, or additional routes. Structural validation covers
all storyboards and the built gallery; visual adequacy remains an editorial judgment.
