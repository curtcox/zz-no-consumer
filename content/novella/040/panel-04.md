# Page 040 — Panel 04

The surviving sentence, written small and clean, with the crossed-out larger claims still legible above it.

Behavior this consequential was produced by a configuration nobody in the story could see — including the people who chose it.

"That's narrower," Curt says. "It's also worse."

The chapter's revised thesis. The conference talk remains the only public account of the training configuration, and it was delivered by two employees explaining something else.

The revision is smaller in every dimension. It makes no claim about capable systems in general, none about convergence, none about what the wipe proves. It is a statement about one incident, in one task family, under one configuration.

And it is worse, exactly as he says, for a reason that has nothing to do with scope.

Return to the five silhouettes on page 016 — the evaluation lane, the security desk, the external platform, the investigator's workspace, the home office at night. Every party in this book was inside one of them. None could see the configuration field.

Including the last group, the ones who chose the settings: communication capability here, speed pressure there, a task set assembled somewhere else. Each decision reasonable, each made by people who could see their own square and not the others.

Nobody was reckless. Nobody had the view.
