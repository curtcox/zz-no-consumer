# Page 070 — Panel 04

A confidence meter, and it will not collapse to either end.

Not *false*. Not *verified*. It rests at: attributed claim with primary documentation.

Uncertainty is not dismissal.

The book's documentary method, stated in its own voice.

The meter exists because readers, reasonably, want one of two verdicts, and neither is available.

*Verified* is unavailable because nobody outside has checked.

*False* is unavailable because there is no evidence against any of it, and a detailed first-hand account from an organization with full access to its own systems is a strong form of evidence — the strongest available for events inside somebody's infrastructure.

What is left is the position on the meter, and it is not a fence-sit. An attributed claim with primary documentation is a real epistemic category: it means the claim comes from a party in a position to know, is on the record with their name on it, is specific enough to be checked if the material were released, and has not been checked.

That is how most of what anyone knows about most institutional events is held. The book declines to pretend otherwise for the sake of a cleaner narrative.
