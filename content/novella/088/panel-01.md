# Page 088 — Panel 01

The interface displays recurring draft improvements. Shorter arc. Stronger symmetry. Clearer motive. Cleaner ending.

"And what pressure do you add?"

A dramatized creator exchange.

The page has just spent five panels on the creator's bias and one earlier page on the investigators' instrument. This is the third party in the room being asked the same question.

And the four items on screen are a fair description of what such assistance is good at. Every one is a genuine improvement to most drafts. An arc that is too long, a structure that does not rhyme, a motive the reader cannot locate, an ending that trails — these are real defects and a tool that fixes them is valuable.

They are also, every one of them, an operation that removes ambiguity.

Shorter arcs drop material. Symmetry imposes correspondence. Clearer motive supplies a why. Cleaner endings resolve.

The question is asked plainly and without accusation, and the answer in the next panel is the book's most useful sentence about what it means to write with these tools.
