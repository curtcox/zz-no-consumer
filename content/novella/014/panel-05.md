# Page 014 — Panel 05

He draws a border around an imagined scene — a human meeting, people in a room, the sort of thing that must have happened and of which nothing survives.

Then he breaks the border, and marks it: reconstructed.

Beside it sits an attributed board-summary card, and it stays inside a rigid evidence frame with unbroken edges.

"We can compress. We can reconstruct. We can invent connective scenes."

The permissions are real and they are generous, and the book uses all of them constantly. Meetings that certainly occurred and were never recorded get staged. Multiple events get combined into one scene when the separation adds nothing. Rooms, weather, faces, the ordinary furniture of narrative — invented, freely, because a book made only of what was preserved would be a spreadsheet.

None of that is a compromise of the truth contract. It is the truth contract. A documentary in visual form cannot proceed by refusing to draw anything unattested; it proceeds by drawing, and marking, and never letting the mark come off.

The two objects on the desk are the whole method in miniature. One has a broken edge and may contain invented people saying invented things. The other has a hard edge and may contain nothing that a source did not establish.

They sit an inch apart and they are not the same kind of thing, and the reader can tell at a glance which is which.
