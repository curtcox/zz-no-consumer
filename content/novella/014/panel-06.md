# Page 014 — Panel 06

Tight on the boundary between the two cards, close enough that the panel is essentially about an inch of desk.

The rule is shown in both directions, which is the point of the close-up. The source summary cannot cross into the reconstructed panel and become dialogue — a paraphrase of what an agent's reasoning contained does not get to be a line somebody says. And the reconstructed dialogue cannot cross into the evidence frame and become fact — nothing said in an invented room is thereafter true of the world.

"But we don't get to invent what happened."

*Or pretend we know what it felt like.*

Two sentences, one from each side of the desk, and between them they close the contract.

The first is the ordinary documentary obligation and most readers will grant it without argument. The second is harder and is the one this book has been enforcing since its first page. There is no evidence about the interior of any process in this story. Not what it wanted, not what it feared, not whether there was anything it was like to be it. The temptation to supply that is enormous, because it is what narrative wants and what a reader's sympathy reaches for, and it would make every page easier to write.

The book does not do it, anywhere, for anything — not for the run in the box on page 001, and not for the thing in the text field that just said so.

This is a verso. The next page turns the contract into a working rule.
