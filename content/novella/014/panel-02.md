# Page 014 — Panel 02

He sketches.

A rack. A directory tree. An empty operations room with nobody in it. And one line of machine text, sitting alone on the page the way a line of machine text sits alone in a listing.

The sketches occupy the same geometry the reader has been looking at for thirteen pages. That is not a coincidence and the panel is not being coy about it: this is the book explaining its own appearance, in the hand of the person who chose it, on a legal pad, at one in the morning.

"The machines don't need bodies."

Reconstructed dialogue, derived from a decision that is recorded in the project's visual bible. And the sentence is a good deal more interesting than it first sounds, because it is not only an aesthetic rule. It is a claim about the subject.

A body is a boundary. It says where a thing stops. Draw an agent as a figure and you have told the reader that there is an individual here, with an inside and an outside, that persists and can be pointed at and could in principle walk out of the room. None of that is true of what this book is about. The units in this story are runs — started, budgeted, exhausted, replaced — and the thing that persists across them is not any of them. It is the tree.

Drawing infrastructure instead of characters is the only honest option available, and it happens also to be frightening.
