# Page 092 — Panel 01

A multistate letter enters the public record, headed: 3 August 2026 · fifteen states.

Three tabs are visible. Preserve records. Protect reporting. Restrict the evaluations until controlled.

These are government demands — not adjudicated findings.

Checked against the letter itself: a preservation demand covering eleven categories of material; a demand that no personnel face adverse action for protected whistleblowing; and a demand that the laboratory cease evaluations prompting models toward advanced exploitation unless and until it can show they are run in a controlled and responsible way.

Fifteen attorneys general, three and a half weeks after the shutdown.

And the caption's distinction is the page's discipline. A demand is a thing an official body asks for. A finding is a thing established through a process with evidence and a decision.

The letter's own account of the incident is drawn from public reporting — the same reports this book is drawn from — which is precisely why its status is demand rather than finding. Nobody had adjudicated anything.

The second tab is the one that reveals what they were worried about. Protecting whistleblowers is what you ask for when you suspect the record you are demanding might not arrive intact.
