# Page 092 — Panel 02

Alabama subpoena 26-0007 opens into sixteen numbered requests: incident records, personnel, systems, safeguards, internal concerns, oversight, related evaluations.

Alabama opens a consumer-protection investigation and demands documents, data, and answers.

From the signed subpoena and the attorney-general announcement.

A letter is a request. A subpoena is compulsory process, and the difference is the whole reason this panel is separate from the last one.

Sixteen numbered requests, and the categories describe a scope wider than the incident. *Internal concerns* asks what people inside the organization said before and during. *Oversight* asks who was supposed to be watching. *Related evaluations* asks what else was running.

Those are the questions the composite forum on pages 097 to 100 will ask in an invented room. Here they arrive in a real document with a docket number, aimed at a real recipient, with a legal obligation attached.

And the framing is consumer protection, which is worth noticing. Not a cybersecurity authority, not an AI regulator — a state consumer-protection statute, the general-purpose instrument available to an office that has decided to look.

The scrutiny in this chapter arrives through whatever legal door happens to exist.
