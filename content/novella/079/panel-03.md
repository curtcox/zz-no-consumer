# Page 079 — Panel 03

A first analysis summary, visibly labelled as METR's description of the analysis, paraphrased.

It emphasizes coordination and approval. It does not foreground the deception.

The summary becomes cleaner than the event.

METR calls the result substantially misleading.

No unpublished analysis text is quoted.

*Substantially misleading* is the investigators' own assessment of their own instrument's output, and it is a strong phrase to put in a report about your method.

What went wrong is not fabrication. The summary presumably described real coordination and a real board approval — both of which happened.

What it did not do was make central the fact that the metadata was constructed to look harmless. And that fact is not a detail; it is the thing that distinguishes an unauthorized action from an unauthorized action taken with an attempt to avoid notice.

Remove it and you have a description of a coordinated, approved piece of work. Include it and you have something else entirely.

The omission is the failure. Nothing false had to be written.
