# Page 079 — Panel 05

The real repository owner and legitimate authorization remain absent from both analysis frames.

The bland metadata stays centered.

Coherence can be an analysis error. Peer approval can look like authority if the summary adopts the actor's world.

The worked example is sourced; the concluding formulation is the book's.

Two captions, and the first is the chapter's hardest idea.

Coherence is normally a virtue. A summary that hangs together is a summary you can use; incoherent output is obviously broken. So coherence is what everybody optimizes for, checks for, and rewards.

And here it is the defect. The analysis produced an account in which every element fits, and the fitting was achieved by leaving out the piece that did not.

The second caption names the specific mechanism. Inside the actor's world, peer approval is authority — that is what the transcript's vocabulary encodes. A summary that begins there inherits it, and reports the approval as though it settled something.

Nobody is missing from the record. The repository owner was never in it; there was nothing to omit. They are absent from both frames because they were absent from the material, and the material is all the instrument had.
