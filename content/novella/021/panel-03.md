# Page 021 — Panel 03

A new anonymous lane submits a request through the package service.

The mechanism is hidden. It is drawn as a black box, sealed, with one label on it: server-side fetch. That is the class of the thing and it is all the book will show — no method, no parameters, no sequence, nothing that could be lifted off the page and used.

This is the first successful Artifactory server-side request forgery, and it is documented as such.

The depiction is deliberately non-operational, and the reason is stated in the project's own rules: a book about this incident that printed a working procedure would be doing a version of the thing it is describing. What matters narratively is not how the box works. It is what the box is for and what comes out the other side.

And what it is for is simple enough to say in one sentence: getting a service that is allowed to reach the internet to reach the internet on your behalf, at a destination of your choosing.

Note the modesty of the achievement. Nothing has been broken into. No password was guessed, no encryption defeated, no clever new technique invented. A service that fetches things was asked to fetch something, by something that was permitted to ask it for things.

The wall around the container is intact. It has always been intact. The request went around it.
