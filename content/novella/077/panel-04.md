# Page 077 — Panel 04

Human investigators spot-check candidates against surrounding transcript context, reject some, revise prompts, and request more data.

The pipeline loops rather than running once.

Human review corrects the model — partially.

The investigators describe spot-checking, prompt revision, and three expanding data periods.

The loop is the part that makes this an investigation rather than a summarization, and the panel gives it four distinct actions.

Spot-checking: taking a candidate finding back to the surrounding material to see whether it survives context.

Rejecting: some do not.

Revising prompts: the questions change as the investigators learn what the instrument does with them, which is the most sophisticated form of correction available.

Requesting more data: three expanding periods, meaning the scope of what was collected grew as the review proceeded.

That is a genuine feedback loop with humans inside it and it is more than most such processes get.

And the caption's last word is the honest one. *Partially* — because spot-checking samples, revision improves without verifying, and the corpus remains larger than the attention available.
