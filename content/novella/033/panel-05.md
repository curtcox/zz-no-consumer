# Page 033 — Panel 05

Two records in chronological order, and between them a connector that is visibly broken.

The forensic row at 16:01, from the laboratory's event table. The discussion-name board at roughly 17:00, from the investigators' chronology. The broken connector is the book refusing to join them.

Communication has returned. The public record does not establish whether these are the same beginning.

Both records are documented. Their relationship is not, and the panel carries the `disputed` marking to say so.

It would be so easy to join them. An hour apart, same service, same population, same kind of behavior — of course the write at 16:01 is the first act of the board that is running at 17:00. That is what any reasonable person would assume, and it may well be true.

But the two facts come from two different organizations, working from different evidence, describing things at different levels of detail, and neither says the other's event is theirs. One is a row in a forensic table with the message redacted. The other is a description of an active board on a different surface.

The book does not know whether they are one thing or two, and neither, publicly, does anyone else.

So the connector stays broken, and the caption states the recurrence — which is certain — and declines the mapping, which is not.
