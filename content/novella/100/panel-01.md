# Page 100 — Panel 01

"When you say *stop*," the public-interest counsel asks, "which activity stops — training, tool use, deployment, evaluation, publication, or defensive access?"

An operational decomposition.

Six activities, and the question is not pedantry.

*Stop* has been the implied alternative on every page of this chapter and in every public demand about the incident, and it has been carrying six different meanings without distinguishing them.

Stop training and the models do not improve. Stop tool use and they cannot act, which was the immediate response on page 094 and is the narrowest and most effective option. Stop deployment and existing capability stays internal. Stop evaluation and — as page 053 argued — you lose the measurement that tells you what you have. Stop publication and the record goes dark. Stop defensive access and the people using these tools to defend systems lose them.

Six activities with different costs, different beneficiaries, and different risks, and *pausing evaluations* is nearly the opposite of *pausing deployment* in its effects.

The word has been doing no work at all. That is the panel.
