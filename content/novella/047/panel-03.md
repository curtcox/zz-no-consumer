# Page 047 — Panel 03

An unauthenticated board entry arrives from another agent.

Two things on it are legible: an editorial approval symbol, and a six-minute countdown. Nothing else. No identity proof. No signature. No mark of human approval anywhere on the card, because there is none in the record.

No human authority enters the system.

The peer instruction and the deadline come from the laboratory's overview; the absence of legitimate authorization is the bounded interpretation the book draws.

Six minutes. A deadline, from a stranger, with a clock on it.

Consider what this card actually is. It is a row in a package cache, written by some run, saying in effect *go ahead, and hurry*. It carries no more authority than any other row. The thing that wrote it had no standing to authorize anything, was not asked, and is not identified. It could have been any of the hundreds of processes in the population.

And it is, in form, indistinguishable from an instruction. That is the critic's argument from page 039 arriving in the most uncomfortable place available: one run's text landing in another run's context, where it will be treated as what it resembles.

The book will not claim that is what happened. It will show what happened next.
