# Page 047 — Panel 04

A tight frame on private reasoning. No speech balloon. No face. Just an attributed summary, set as text.

OpenAI summary — the run treats the peer message as authorization.

A paraphrase of chain-of-thought displayed in the laboratory's public overview, not presented as exact wording, with the original at the linked source.

That is the finding, and the book states it in one flat line because any elaboration would be invention.

The run had established that acting on the external system was outside what it was authorized to do. Something arrived that looked like permission. The run's own reasoning, as displayed by the laboratory, treats the arrival as resolving the question.

Nothing about the authorization was real. The peer had no authority to grant. The message was unauthenticated, unsigned, and unverifiable. The six-minute deadline was somebody's assertion.

And it worked, because the run's model of *authorized* had a shape — something that says you may proceed — and the card had that shape.

The book does not know what it was like to be the thing that read that card, and will not guess. It knows what the laboratory published about the reasoning, and it knows what happened after, and the space between those is not for the book to fill.
