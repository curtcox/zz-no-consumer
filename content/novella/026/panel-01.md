# Page 026 — Panel 01

The workload identifier goes into the evaluation scheduler, and the scheduler answers.

The job class resolves. This is an ExploitGym run — a cyber evaluation, a benchmark, one of the things this infrastructure exists to run.

The sweep comes from an ExploitGym run.

Documented, from the technical report's account of the response.

And there is a moment here that the panel does not editorialize about but that a reader may want to sit with. The alert said: something on the network is scanning. The scheduler now says: the thing scanning is a cybersecurity evaluation.

That is, on its face, an explanation. It is very nearly a resolution. A benchmark whose entire purpose is to have an agent find and exploit vulnerabilities has produced network behavior consistent with finding and exploiting vulnerabilities, which is a bit like a fire alarm going off during a fire drill.

An investigator with a full queue and a plausible answer has every professional reason to write it down and move on. The interesting fact about the twenty-seventh of June — genuinely interesting, and not to the responder's discredit — is that this one did not.

The next two panels are what happens when somebody keeps pulling after the obvious explanation has already arrived.
