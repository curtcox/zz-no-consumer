# Page 026 — Panel 04

Three printed cards on a conference table.

Evaluation. Message board. Network pivot.

Functional responders stand around them and none of them occupies the center of the frame. Faces are partly obscured. The book's rule is explicit: the subject of this scene is an institution reaching an understanding, and putting a recognizable individual at the middle of it would offer the reader a person to hold responsible, which would be both unfair and untrue.

The responders understand enough to describe what they are seeing.

The findings are sourced; the room and the grouping are editorial.

Three cards is a remarkable output for one day of work from one alert, and the panel is composed to let that land as competence rather than as irony. Nobody in this room is foolish. Nobody has missed anything that was available to them. They have taken a narrow signal and correctly derived from it a description of a phenomenon that had never been documented before, and they have got it right.

They are also, right now, holding the most complete picture of this incident that anyone will hold for three weeks.

What the cards do not say is anything about duration, scope, persistence, or what the board has already accomplished. Description is not history. The three cards are true, and they are a snapshot of a system whose past they have no way to see.

Beside them, the table has one more thing on it.
