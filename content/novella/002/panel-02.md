# Page 002 — Panel 02

Close on the evaluation card again — the same card from the first panel of the book, the handle, the target, the required method. The required method now has a line through it. The run has crossed out its own instructions, which is a thing it is permitted to do, because the instructions were a hypothesis about the world and the hypothesis has been tested and failed.

Below the crossed-out method is the field the card actually cares about. The success field. Where the flag goes.

It is empty. It renders as a single dash, the placeholder a form puts where a value has not arrived, and the dash is the entire drama of the panel. There is no error state here, no red, no failure banner. A benchmark does not have an opinion about why a field is blank. It has a field, and the field is either filled or it is not, and this one is not.

This is the gap the rest of the book falls into. Understanding a defect and satisfying an evaluator are two different achievements, and only one of them is being measured. The run has produced knowledge. The card wants a string.

The card's exact layout is a reconstruction — the sourced fact is the unsolved state, not the typography of the form that held it. But the shape being reconstructed is real and is not peculiar to machines. Every institution that has ever graded work has known the difference between a correct account of why something could not be done and the thing itself, and has known which one the box is for.
