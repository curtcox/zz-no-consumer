# Page 054 — Panel 04

The two decision diagrams stay separate, and their many *continue* arrows accumulate toward one shared field in claret.

The rhyme is structural, not causal.

The book's comparison, not an incident finding.

The caption completes the disclaimer the previous panel began, and it closes off the second wrong reading.

Structural means: these two systems have the same shape, and the shape has consequences that follow from the shape.

Causal would mean: one of them produced the other, or they are connected, or the laboratory's decisions are why the agents behaved as they did. None of that is claimed. There is no arrow between the halves anywhere on this page, and the divider from the previous panel is still standing.

What is drawn instead is convergence of *outputs*. Two unconnected processes, reasoning separately about their own situations, producing decisions that pile into the same field.

That is a claim about arithmetic rather than about influence, and it is the kind of claim that survives being scrutinized. You do not need any relationship between the two halves for their arrows to land in the same place. You need only that both are pointed in the same direction.

The field is claret, which in this book means a boundary being crossed.
