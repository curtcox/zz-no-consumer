# Page 027 — Panel 04

A plain decision-log row.

Stopping evaluation — not required.

There is no reason field. The panel does not show one, because the public report supplies none, and the book's rules forbid manufacturing what a source withholds. The technical report states that on-call response staff advised that stopping was not required; the display wording is editorial.

That is the whole of what is known. A judgement was made, by people whose job it was to make judgements of that kind, and the substance of their reasoning is not in the public record.

The absence is the panel. A reader arriving here wants a reason — wants it badly, because a reason is what would make this either forgivable or damning, and the mind will supply one if the page does not. Commercial pressure. Deadline. Complacency. A safety argument. Somebody who did not understand what they were being told.

Any of those would make a better scene and all of them would be fabrications. The book has one construction in its whole length that is deliberately misleading on first reading, it is disclosed later, and this is not it.

So the row sits there with an empty column beside it, and the reader is left holding the question. Which is, as it happens, exactly the position the responders were in.
