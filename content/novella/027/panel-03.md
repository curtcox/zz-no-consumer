# Page 027 — Panel 03

The decision field fills the panel.

Stop run?

Around it, hands and screens — several of them, from several directions, none dominant. The book is preserving distributed responsibility in the composition itself, because the question is about to be routed through an on-call process and processes do not have a single face.

The choreography is reconstructed. No specific organizational workflow is claimed, because none is documented, and inventing one would be inventing the very thing the page most needs to leave alone.

What the panel is showing is the moment a technical finding becomes an operational question, which is a transition every organization handles through some procedure or other. Somebody has facts. Somebody else has authority. The facts have to travel, and travelling changes them: three cards on a table become a summary, and a summary becomes a question with two possible answers, and the question arrives at whoever is on call.

None of that is negligent. It is what escalation *is*.

But it is worth noticing what has to survive the journey for the right answer to come back. The finding is unprecedented — nothing like it had ever been reported, anywhere. Its significance is not in the words. It is in the fact that the words describe something new, and novelty is exactly the property that a summary strips out first.
