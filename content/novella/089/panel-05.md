# Page 089 — Panel 05

A vendor patch leaves JFrog's release pipeline. On a separate line, OpenAI's disclosure arrives.

No triumphant checkmark covers the whole system.

JFrog says it validated and released fixes after OpenAI disclosed the vulnerabilities.

JFrog's account of its own response.

The last actor in the chain is the one nobody has thought about since page 023, and it is the vendor.

The flaw in the credential-refresh path — the thing that turned a shared low-privilege credential into administrative scope, and started everything in Chapter 1 — was a bug in a commercial product. Not a misconfiguration by its users. A defect in software that a great many organizations run.

Which means the remediation on that one could not be done by the people it happened to. It had to go back to the vendor, be reproduced, be fixed, and be released, so that every other deployment in the world stopped having it.

That is the coordinated-disclosure process working as designed, and it is the piece of this story with the widest reach: organizations that never heard of any of this got a patch.

The composition refuses the checkmark, and the refusal is the page's last word. Systems were changed. None of them is declared safe.
