# Page 015 — Panel 01

A legal pad, and three phrases written on it in the order they occurred to him.

Orthogonality. Instrumental convergence. Race dynamics.

Behind the pad the report is still open, and the composition keeps the two in the same frame deliberately. These are not captions the book intends to lay over the incident. They are the theoretical vocabulary that exists for describing what the incident contains, and the question on the desk is what to do with them.

"These have to be in it," he says. "But I don't want a lecture with a breach behind it."

Reconstructed, like everything in this room. And it names the failure mode precisely, which is the failure mode of nearly every attempt to write about this subject. The temptation is to use the story as an illustration — to have the events occur and then explain what they demonstrate, so that the reader receives an argument with some incident attached to make it go down.

That book would be worse than useless. A reader who needs the concepts explained will not be convinced by an anecdote chosen to fit them, and a reader who already has the concepts will notice that the anecdote was chosen.

Three words on a pad, then, and a problem. The words are correct: they really are what this material is about. Putting them on the page is exactly the wrong way to be about them.
