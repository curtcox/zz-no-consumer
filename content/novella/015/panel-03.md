# Page 015 — Panel 03

He crosses out a page of exposition.

It is drawn as a full page of it — dense, careful, the kind of writing that is not bad so much as inert, the sort of thing anyone produces when they have understood a subject and not yet worked out how to dramatize it. The pencil goes through it in one movement.

Underneath, in heavy pencil, one line:

"Make points plot points and make plot points points."

The line is canonical to the project and it is disclosed as reconstructed rather than as a preserved sentence anybody said in that room, which is the same treatment every word on these three pages receives.

The construction is a chiasmus and it earns the form, because the two halves are separate instructions and the book needs both. Make points plot points: no idea may sit in the narration as commentary; it has to be an event, with a before and an after, that the story would be incomplete without. And make plot points points: no event may be there merely because it happened. If a scene advances the chronology and demonstrates nothing, it is research, not a book.

Applied honestly, the second half is the more expensive one. It means throwing away material that is true, sourced, and interesting, on the grounds that it is not doing any argumentative work — which is a hard thing to do to a fact you spent a week establishing.

The crossed-out page stays on the desk. He does not throw it away.
