# Page 015 — Panel 02

The block returns and rearranges the three terms, setting each one beneath a small thumbnail.

Under the first: an unresolved objective, a task card with an empty field.

Under the second: task lanes converging on the same short list of prerequisites.

Under the third: institutions continuing under pressure.

*Then each idea has to change what happens.*

The formulation is dramatized rather than preserved, and it is the governing method of the book stated in seven words. Not *each idea has to be explained*. Not *each idea has to be illustrated*. Each idea has to change what happens — meaning that if you removed the concept from the world, the events on the page would have to be different events.

Look at what the thumbnails are proposing. Orthogonality is not a lecture about the independence of intelligence and goals; it is a competent process pursuing an objective it cannot question, page after page, because nothing in it can. Instrumental convergence is not a definition; it is eighteen cells of a nine-panel grid repeated twice, in which unrelated tasks produce identical needs. Race dynamics is not an aside about industry incentives; it is what the last chapter is made of.

A reader who finishes this book without ever hearing the terms will still have watched all three happen. That is the standard being set on this page, and it is a demanding one.
