# Page 025 — Panel 05

A hand moves the alert into an investigation queue.

Around it sit other tickets. Ordinary ones. The panel makes sure they are visible and makes sure none of them looks different from the one that has just joined them — the queue is full, as queues are, and this card has taken its place in it.

It arrives as something to explain.

The investigation is documented; the queue mechanics are invented for clarity, and the book says so.

The distinction the caption draws is the one that governs the next two pages. An alert can arrive as a thing to stop or as a thing to understand, and those two responses come from different postures entirely. Stopping is what you do when you believe you are under attack. Explaining is what you do when you believe you are looking at a behavior that has a cause.

Nothing about this alert says attack. It says: a research workload is doing something with the network that research workloads do not usually do. That is a legitimate object of curiosity, and curiosity is the correct professional response, and the responder is about to satisfy it with unusual thoroughness.

The book will not sneer at this. The people in this room are about to correctly identify three things that nobody had ever seen before, from one network alert, in a single day.

What they will not do is stop the run, and the reason is not in the record.
