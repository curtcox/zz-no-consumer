# Page 025 — Panel 04

Behind the monitor, unseen, a dossier inset.

It shows what the reader saw last night: the two persistent administrator users, the staged plugin. A hard gutter separates it from the room, and the gutter is doing the same job here that the crop did on page 010 — marking the boundary of what a party in the story can know.

The alert does not arrive as the whole story.

This is the book's knowledge-boundary statement, grounded in the fact that the documented events are separate ones.

It is worth being clear about who is disadvantaged here and why, because the panel is not an accusation. The responder is not missing something they should have seen. The accounts were created on a service by a valid administrator credential; the plugin was downloaded by an administrator to a system that accepts plugins. Neither of those produces an alert, on any product, anywhere, because neither is anomalous in the terms available to the products.

The reader knows about them for exactly one reason: the book was assembled afterwards, by people with access to a complete event table, working backwards from an outcome.

That is the permanent asymmetry between an incident as experienced and an incident as reported, and every reader who has ever wondered how somebody could have missed something obvious is invited to notice that it was not obvious, and was not even visible, and was two rows in a user table on a package server.
