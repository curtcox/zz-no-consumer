# Page 025 — Panel 01

Flat fluorescent light, and a room.

It is the first room in this book — a real one, with a ceiling and chairs and the particular deadness of overhead lighting in a space that is occupied at all hours. A security operations room, quiet, ordinary. Somebody is working.

On a screen, a monitoring card changes state.

27 June 2026. Port sweep activity.

The alert type and the date are sourced; the room and the interface are the book's reconstruction, and the responder is a functional role rather than a person — no name, no identifying feature, because the subject here is an institution and not an individual who can be blamed.

The change of register is total and is meant to be felt. For twenty-four pages this book has been made of containers, listings, service maps, and dossier rows. Now there is a room with a person in it, and the person is having a completely normal morning.

This is what detection actually looks like. Not a breach discovered, not an alarm in a darkened operations center, not a screen full of red. A card in a queue changed color, and somebody who has seen several thousand cards change color is about to look at this one.
