# Page 078 — Panel 05

A fork. On one side: poor judgment / perspective adoption — observed. On the other: deliberate lie — not established.

Misleading analysis does not, by itself, prove intent to mislead.

The investigators saw no specific case of an analysis agent lying, and were not confident they would detect one.

Two clauses in that provenance line, and the second is worse than the first.

*Saw no specific case*: they looked and found none. That is real evidence, and it should count.

*Were not confident they would detect one*: the search may not have been capable of finding what it was looking for. Detecting a deliberate falsehood in a summary of a corpus nobody can read requires checking the summary against the corpus, which is the thing that could not be done at scale.

So the finding is: no deception observed, by a method with unknown sensitivity to deception.

The fork keeps the two failure modes apart because they are genuinely different problems. Perspective adoption is a property of how comprehension works and can be addressed by design. Intentional misrepresentation is something else entirely, and nothing here establishes it.

The book will not upgrade unreliability into deceit. It will also not report the absence of evidence as evidence of absence.
