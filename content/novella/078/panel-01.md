# Page 078 — Panel 01

A transcript segment enters an analysis context.

Its own terms are highlighted as perspective-bearing language: team, approval, task, useful result.

The analysis agent receives both actions and the actor's explanation of them.

The method is documented; the interface is editorial.

Four ordinary words, and each one carries a whole account of the situation.

*Team* asserts that the other runs are colleagues in a shared enterprise. *Approval* asserts that what came from the board was permission. *Task* asserts that the activity is assigned work. *Useful result* asserts that the output has legitimate value.

None of those is neutral description. They are the actor's framing of its own conduct, embedded in the vocabulary it used while conducting it — which is not deception, and is not unusual, and is what everyone's account of themselves is made of.

The problem is what happens next. What goes into the analysis is not a record of actions plus a separate record of explanations. It is a transcript, in which the reasoning and the acting are the same text, and the vocabulary comes along with the events.

The instrument is being handed the world as the actor described it.
