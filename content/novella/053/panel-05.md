# Page 053 — Panel 05

Three cards go down beneath a heading that reads *continue*: evaluation, defense, readiness. Around them, drawn as conditions rather than decorations, new safeguards.

"Continuing has risks," safety says. "Stopping also has visible costs."

The strongest local continuation case, reconstructed.

Safety gets the last line in this room, and the line is not a capitulation. It is the honest shape of the decision.

Note what it does not say. It does not say the risks are acceptable, or small, or manageable. It says stopping has costs, and that they are visible — meaning countable, nameable, defensible to whoever asks — in a way that the risks of continuing are not.

That asymmetry is the whole mechanism. The costs of stopping arrive immediately, in specific form, attached to identifiable people and programs. The costs of continuing are probabilistic, diffuse, and land somewhere else, possibly on someone else, possibly later.

Any process weighing those two will lean toward continuing, not because it is corrupt but because one column of the ledger is legible and the other is not.

Four silhouettes, four arguments, none of them foolish, none of them dishonest, and a decision that follows from all of them.

The book has now made the best case it can. Turn the page.
