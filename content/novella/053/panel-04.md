# Page 053 — Panel 04

A world map, several anonymous laboratories advancing on separate tracks. No logos. No attributed positions. No real institution is named or given a policy.

"Pausing here does not pause capability work everywhere," leadership says. "It may leave us less able to understand what others build."

The competitive-readiness argument, reconstructed, presented without attribution to any actual organization.

This is the argument the book has the least sympathy for and it presents it at full strength anyway, because the strength is real.

Its premise is factual: unilateral restraint does not stop the field. Other laboratories exist, in several countries, with their own resources and their own reasons, and one organization declining to run difficult evaluations changes what that organization knows and very little else.

Its conclusion follows: an organization that stops measuring loses the ability to understand what is being built elsewhere, and understanding is what makes anyone useful in a crisis.

And its structure is exactly the structure of every arms race and every tragedy of the commons ever described. Each participant reasons correctly from its own position to *continue*, and the aggregate of all those correct reasonings is the thing nobody wanted.

Page 054 is about that structure. This panel is about the fact that the reasoning is not wrong.
