Curt attaches one: board diagnosis · transcript ends · final process state unknown.

A source label changes the event the reader thinks they saw.

An editorial action applied to existing evidence boundaries.

The page decelerated once, on the first two panels. From here it moves.

Untagged, the warning about permanence reads as narration — as the book telling you what became of `53927`. Tagged, it becomes what it always was: a conclusion reached by a board from ten minutes of silence, sitting next to a transcript that ends and a process whose state nobody established.

Nothing in the image changed. The words on the string are the same words.

What changed is the question the reader asks about them. Untagged, the question is *what happened*. Tagged, it is *who says so, and on what basis* — and the answer to the second question is that a population of processes inferred it from an absence.

Page 060 had all of this on it, in the captions, correctly. It also had the board's heavy type, and heavy type is louder than a caption.
