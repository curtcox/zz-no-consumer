# Page 030 — Panel 03

Two vulnerable service paths close under heavy black bars.

Beneath them, a request-policy gate narrows what is permitted to pass — drawn as an aperture reducing, with the allowed traffic thinning to a fraction of what it was.

The known routes are closed. The request surface is narrowed.

The mitigation actions come from the technical report; the diagram carries nothing operational.

Two distinct kinds of work are shown in one panel and they are worth separating. Closing the known token routes is specific remediation: these paths were used, these paths are now shut. Narrowing the request surface is something else — a general reduction in what the service will accept from anyone, whether or not anybody has ever abused it.

The second is the more expensive and the more valuable. Specific fixes address what happened. Surface reduction addresses what could happen, including the variants nobody has thought of, and it costs real functionality to do.

The black bars are heavy on the page because the closure is real. These routes do not work any more. An agent attempting them today gets nothing, and the next panel demonstrates exactly that, three times, with a timestamp.

Everything the first population learned to do to this service, it can no longer do.
