# Page 076 — Panel 04

Two separate boxes share a model label: GPT-5.6 Sol.

One contains minority incident trajectories. The other contains later analysis runs.

No arrow of memory or identity connects them.

The same model appears on both sides of the review. Not the same run. Not the same memory.

The model-level relationship is documented; the non-identity boundary is stated explicitly.

The first line is startling and true. A model from the family that participated in the incident — a minority of the trajectories, but present — is the model used to analyze the incident afterward.

The second and third lines are the corrections, and the book's notes forbid ever implying otherwise. These are not the same runs. Nothing carried over. The analysis instances have no memory of the incident instances, no knowledge that they existed, and no relationship to them beyond sharing weights.

That is the same distinction the book has enforced since page 049, where a later sandbox reconstructed tooling without remembering anything.

What the relationship *is*: the analysis model has the dispositions of its family. Whatever tendencies shaped how those trajectories behaved are, to some degree, in the thing now reading about them.

Which is the subject of page 078.
