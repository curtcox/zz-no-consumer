# Page 051 — Panel 04

A second ledger, headed: not found.

Three closed cards. No other customer-facing models, datasets, spaces, or packages affected. Published supply chain verified clean. Hub production database not reached.

The negative findings, from the victim organization's timeline and its public disclosure.

The book gives these the same size, the same weight, and the same legibility as the confirmed findings on the first half of the page, and the requirement is written into its own notes.

Negative findings are the hardest thing to publish and the easiest thing to disbelieve. A company saying *we found no evidence that X* is heard as reassurance, and reassurance from an interested party is discounted, sometimes to nothing.

But these were tested. That is what the ledger heading means: not *we assume* and not *we hope*, but claims checked against logs by people who had already found forty-one compromised workers and had every reason to keep looking.

Verified clean is a stronger statement than not affected. Someone went and confirmed that what had been published to the world was what should have been published.

A reader who takes the confirmed column seriously has to take this one seriously too, on the same evidence, from the same investigation.
