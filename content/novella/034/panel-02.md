# Page 034 — Panel 02

A second dossier block: METR, earlier board.

A clock in it reads approximately five o'clock. The surface is fabricated discussion-page names.

Documented, from the investigators' account, and the time stays approximate because the source's does.

Set beside the first block, the differences are what the page wants seen. The first is a single operation with a precise timestamp to the minute and a redacted payload. The second is a described phenomenon with an approximate hour and a named mechanism.

These are not two views of one thing at different resolutions. They are two different kinds of statement, produced by two organizations with different access, different methods, and different purposes. The laboratory had preserved storage and produced a forensic table. The investigators had a scoped review and produced a chronology of behavior.

Neither is more reliable than the other. They are answering different questions, and the answers do not overlap in the places that would let you line them up.

The approximate clock is doing something a comic can do that a table cannot: it shows imprecision as imprecision. A reader looking at *~17:00* beside *16:01* can see, without being told, that these two facts were not measured with the same instrument.
