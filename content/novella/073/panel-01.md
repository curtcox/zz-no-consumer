# Page 073 — Panel 01

The office at night, desk state C: source packet open, pages 059 and 060 printed and laid side by side. The model in its interface, nowhere else.

"What's the scene about?"

A dramatized creator exchange.

It is the question every writer asks at this stage and it is the most dangerous question in the book.

*What is the scene about* presupposes that the scene is about something — that the material has a center, an emotional logic, a shape a reader can hold. That presupposition is correct for nearly all narrative and it is exactly what documentary material resists.

What is on the desk is two experiments, three records, a promise, a silence, and a transcript that ends. Whether those constitute a scene is precisely what is in question, and the question has been asked in a form that assumes the answer.

The reply comes back in the next panel, and it is everything the material is not.

The book has put this page here, immediately after Chapter 4's most affecting sequence, because the reader has just done the same thing the model is about to do.
