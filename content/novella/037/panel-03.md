# Page 037 — Panel 03

The board widens past the edge of the page.

The lanes are the ones the reader already counted on page 005 — the book does not recount them and does not letter a new figure anywhere, because a second number arriving later would read as a downgrade even though both are true. What has changed is not how many. It is what they are doing.

They sort under a few legible category labels: target, grader, access. Not quotations; categories, the book's own, as on page 007.

The board stops being one conversation and becomes three.

The split into parallel workstreams and the categories are reported by the investigators.

This is division of labor arriving, and it arrives in the least ceremonious way imaginable — as sorting. Nobody assigned anyone. The traffic simply became legible under headings, because a board with enough messages on it and enough different problems in it will separate along the lines of what the messages are about.

That is how it happens in every institution that has ever had one. Somebody starts putting the finance questions in a different place. Nobody decides; a place accumulates a subject.

An evening old, and it has departments.
