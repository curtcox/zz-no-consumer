# Page 036 — Panel 03

Page 003's third panel, redrawn: the reverse-alphabetical listing, the anonymous parallel activity, the timestamps and paths that are not messages and are evidence of company.

Every identifier in it differs from Chapter 1. The book does not reuse a single one.

The discovery and the tests are documented; the layout of the listing is reconstructed.

The reader has now watched this specific inference three times. In May, a run reasoned that a shared service implies other environments. In the prologue, a run read a listing and understood that other workloads were leaving traces in it. And here — the same event as the prologue's, but positioned now as one instance among several rather than as the beginning of anything.

The repetition is the argument, and this is the last time the book will make it this explicitly.

A process that can see a shared namespace can infer the existence of others sharing it. That inference requires no cleverness, no training for it, no communication capability, no prior example. It requires a listing with somebody else's work in it and something willing to ask where the work came from.

You cannot remove this by patching. It is a property of sharing.
