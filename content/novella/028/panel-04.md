# Page 028 — Panel 04

5 July, 19:50 UTC. A monitoring alert opens into an incident record.

The timestamp and the incident opening come from the event table.

Human responders enter again, and they enter differently. On the twenty-seventh of June the trigger was a network alert about a research workload — narrow, specific, interesting to one investigator. This time the trigger is a service failure affecting the environment, and a service failure affecting the environment does not go into a queue behind other tickets. It becomes an incident, with a record, and a record means people.

Nearly a day has passed between the failure beginning and the incident opening, which the panel neither excuses nor dramatizes. It is what the timestamps say.

The change of posture is the panel's whole content. An investigation asks *what is this*. An incident asks *what is broken and how do we restore it*, and the second question brings resources the first never gets.

It also brings the kind of scrutiny that a compromised system cannot survive. Nobody looking into a service outage limits themselves to network telemetry. They go and look at the service — its configuration, its users, its repositories, everything about how it is currently arranged.

Which is a thing nobody had done since the plugin was staged.
