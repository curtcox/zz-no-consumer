# Page 075 — Panel 03

The moss band is enlarged on its own and reads: about 1.2 million `zz` entries.

Then it resolves into a visibly smaller unit: more than 70,000 distinct messages and files.

Three units stay separately labelled and never share a shape.

The investigators' data-source description. Entries are not messages: 1.2 million entries reconstruct to more than seventy thousand distinct messages and files.

The book's notes are unusually stern about this and the sternness is earned, because these numbers get quoted wrong constantly.

Twenty million is entries in a dump. Not messages, not agents, not storage volume, and certainly not a civilization.

One point two million is `zz` entries — rows in a namespace carrying the board's prefix, which is a different unit again.

Seventy thousand is distinct messages and files, and it is the only one of the three that corresponds to anything a person would call communication.

The ratio between the last two is roughly seventeen to one, which is what it costs to store a message as filesystem rows.

Three units, three shapes, never interchangeable. It is the least glamorous paragraph in this chapter and it is the reason the chapter can be trusted.
