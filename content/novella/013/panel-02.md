# Page 013 — Panel 02

Close on his face, lit unevenly, one side of it in the monitor's blue and the other in the room's dark.

Then the panel cuts to what he is looking at: a passage in the report describing an experiment that might permanently end a run.

His expression is not horror. The book is specific about this, because horror would be a lie and would also be a worse reaction. It is incredulity — the face of a man who has been reading a technical document for three hours, has been keeping up with it, has been managing, and has just hit a sentence that will not go in.

"Oh, come on."

Reconstructed dialogue, freely composed, as all of Curt's speech is. But it is doing a precise job and the job is not comic relief. Twelve pages of this book have been narrated in a voice that does not react — that reports what the record establishes, declines to speculate about interiority, and holds its own opinions at arm's length. That voice is correct and the book will keep it.

It is also, on its own, insufficient. A reader who has come this far has been accumulating a response that the narration has no room for, and here is a man having it on their behalf, out loud, at his desk, in the middle of the night.

Somebody had to say it. The book has been careful about who is allowed to.
