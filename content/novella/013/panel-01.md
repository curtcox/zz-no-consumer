# Page 013 — Panel 01

Everything changes.

The border of the panel is broken — a deliberate, visible break, the book's mark for a scene it has assembled rather than found. Inside it is a home office, late at night, and the palette has gone from charcoal and steel blue to nicotine beige and the weak blue of a monitor throwing light on a face.

Curt is at the desk. Ordinary clothes, the kind you are wearing at one in the morning because you never changed out of them. A drink at his elbow, half gone. Far too many browser tabs, in the manner of a person who has been following something for hours and has not closed anything in case he needs it again.

One screen holds the investigators' report. Another holds a timeline, and an editorial summary of the request that started the board.

He has just reached the passage about where the board came from.

The scene is a reconstruction and the broken border says so. The documents on the screens are real and are cited; the room, the hour, the drink, the arrangement of the windows are composed. The book's rules permit this — the creator's domestic detail may be freely built — on the condition that it never pretends to be preserved autobiography.

What is not reconstructed is that somebody read this material and could not leave it alone.
