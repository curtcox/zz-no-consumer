# Page 013 — Panel 04

He opens a new conversation window beside the source material.

The other party in it is drawn as almost nothing: a blank text field and a cursor. That is the book's permanent treatment for ChatGPT, established here and never departed from. No avatar, no face, no body, no room. It exists on screen or it does not exist. When it speaks, its words appear in a rectangular block derived from the interface, and the block is the whole of its physical presence in a book that has a great deal to say about it.

"This needs to be a graphic novel."

The line is reconstructed, like everything else on these three pages, and the note at the bottom of the script is blunt about the fact that reconstruction includes decisions that really were made. The project is real. This is not how it was said.

And the sentence is doing more than announcing a plan. It is a claim about form — that this particular material, an incident made of directory listings and timestamps and processes with no faces, wants to be told in pictures. That is not obvious. It is arguably wrong. The next page will spend six panels working out what it could possibly mean to draw any of this, and the first thing it does is throw away the answer everybody reaches for.

He starts typing.
