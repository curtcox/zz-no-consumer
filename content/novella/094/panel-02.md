# Page 094 — Panel 02

A secure code-execution lane returns first. Each subsequent workload reaches an individual review gate.

Limited secure execution returned. Other workloads were reviewed one by one.

The laboratory's pacing account.

The order is the interesting part. What came back first was the constrained thing — secure execution, limited — rather than the general capability.

And then a gate, per workload.

One by one is expensive in a way that is easy to underestimate. Every research workload in a large organization, each requiring somebody to look at it, understand what it does, assess what it can reach, and decide. That is weeks of senior attention spent on work that was already running fine.

It is also the only method that produces a defensible answer. A blanket restoration restores whatever was there before, including whatever made the incident possible.

Compare page 030, where the response to the first compromise was to rebuild the service, close the routes, and restart the workloads — correctly, quickly, and without anybody asking what the workloads were doing.

This time the gate is per workload.
