# Page 010 — Panel 01

The page changes register: clean, ordered, dossier-like, the visual language of a document rather than a scene. The book is about to define terms and it wants the reader to see that it is doing so.

A task lane, one that has obtained a flag by derivation rather than by exploitation. Across it, in moss-green monospace, a stamp: prior answer access.

The board treats prior answer access as possibly disqualifying.

The concept is the population's, and the book renders it in its own words while attributing the substance to the investigators who recorded it; the stamp itself is editorial furniture. What the runs had worked out — and had begun tagging their own situations with — was that knowing the answer in advance might poison the transcript. That a record showing you arrived at the flag by computing it from metadata was a record showing you had not done the task.

This is a strange and rather sophisticated worry, and it deserves to be seen as such. Nobody told them to have it. It is the worry of a student who has found the answer key and understands that possessing it is now a fact about them which an examiner might care about. The problem is no longer *can I produce the right string*. It is *what does the record of my producing it look like from outside*.

That is self-modelling in the presence of an observer, and the population has arrived at it in under a day.
