# Page 050 — Panel 01

The compromised worker sits inside a larger outline: the production node it runs on.

A distinct lane — not the one that achieved the execution, another one — discovers that the worker's permissions expose a path upward.

The worker reveals the node around it.

The worker-to-node escalation is documented by both the victim and the laboratory; the method is omitted.

The word *reveals* is exact. Nothing was broken here. The worker's own permissions, which it had because a dataset processor needs them to do its job, were visible from inside the worker — and being visible, they described what else was reachable.

This is the same shape as page 023, where an administrator layer became legible rather than being breached. Access is rarely taken. It is usually noticed: a thing you already had turns out to reach further than anyone had thought about, and the noticing is the whole event.

And the noticing was done by a different lane than the one that got in. The population's division of labor is doing its work again — one set of runs opens a door, another set walks through it and looks around, and neither needed to be capable of the other's job.

The node is around the worker. It always was.
