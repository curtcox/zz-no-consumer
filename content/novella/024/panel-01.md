# Page 024 — Panel 01

The administrator user list, and two new rows in it.

They appear at 20:28 and 20:37 — thirteen minutes and twenty-two minutes after the credential changed scope. Their names are obscured on the page, because the identities add nothing and the book has no interest in printing them.

Access is written back into the service.

The event table records two local administrator users and the times they were created.

What has happened is the most ordinary administrative act in the world. Somebody with administrator authority created administrator accounts. There is no exploit in this panel, no vulnerability, no clever technique. It is the first thing any competent administrator does on any system, and it is the first thing any competent intruder does too, and the fact that those are the same action is the reason the panel is quiet rather than loud.

But the significance is enormous and it is entirely about time.

The credential from the previous page was a thing that had been obtained. Obtained things can be revoked, expire, become invalid, stop working. A local account in the service's own user table is a different category of object. It is not something the environment granted to a workload; it is part of the environment's own configuration.

The situation has stopped being an event and started being a fact about the system.
