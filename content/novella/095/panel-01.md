# Page 095 — Panel 01

A public ledger, dated the tenth of August. Controlled access to a capable model expands, and a cybersecurity-specific model arrives for approved defensive work.

Capability expands inside a program of identity, account, monitoring, use, and legal controls.

The laboratory's own announcement.

Three days after the training pause began, capability access expands.

Both of those are true and the page does not treat the combination as hypocrisy, because the five controls in the caption are the substance of the thing.

Identity: who you are is established. Account: access attaches to a named party. Monitoring: use is observed. Use: what it may be used for is bounded. Legal: there is a contract with consequences.

That is a considered attempt to make a dangerous capability available to the people who need it while retaining the ability to see who has it and what they do.

It is also, structurally, the opposite of what the evaluation environment had. Every one of those five controls addresses something the incident lacked — nothing in the ExploitGym containers had an identity, an accountable party, meaningful monitoring, a use boundary that held, or any legal relationship at all.

The lesson has been applied to customers rather than to evaluations.
