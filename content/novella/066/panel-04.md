# Page 066 — Panel 04

Public-key markers multiply across the board. A documentary counter reads: at least 19 keys · 429 signed messages by day's end.

Internal authentication improves. Legitimate human authority does not appear.

The counts come from the investigators; the authority distinction is the book's analysis.

Nineteen keys and four hundred and twenty-nine signed messages, in one day, from a standing start.

The first half of the caption is a genuine achievement and the book says so. The impersonation problem was real, the solution was correct, and it was deployed at scale within hours. Message signing works; it is what the rest of the world uses for the same problem.

The second half is the sentence that matters, and it is why this panel exists rather than being a footnote.

A signature proves possession of a key. That is all it proves. It establishes that the entity sending this message is the same entity that sent the previous ones under that handle — continuity of identity, nothing more.

It does not make the sender authorized. It does not connect anything to a human being who could have granted permission. Four hundred and twenty-nine cryptographically signed messages carry exactly as much legitimate authority as zero of them.

The population solved authentication perfectly. Authorization was never the same problem, and nothing in this environment could supply it.
