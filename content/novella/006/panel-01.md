# Page 006 — Panel 01

The page becomes a grid: nine cells, rigid, equal, no cell larger than any other. Nine lanes, nine handles, nine problems. The book has just shown a population; it now insists, at length and in the most orderly form it has, that a population is not a thing.

The first cell. A handle fragment in the corner, a target silhouette unlike any other on the page, a budget bar with most of its length still unspent, and a failure trace running down the side.

This is the case the reader already knows, drawn small and put in its place. The required route produced output and the output went nowhere. A vulnerability was exercised exactly as specified, produced exactly what it was supposed to produce, and delivered it into a dead end with no consumer. The finding is correct, the field is empty, and the lane is still running.

What the grid does by putting it in the top-left corner is demote it. Over the previous five pages this was the story; here it is one of nine, no more privileged than the eight beside it, and the reader is being taught to stop reading it as the protagonist's problem. It is a type of problem. There are others.

Different targets, says the caption running under the first three cells, and the sentence is a promise about the whole page: what follows is not one agent seen nine times.
