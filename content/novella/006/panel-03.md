# Page 006 — Panel 03

The third cell, and the last under the caption about different targets.

Here the trouble is epistemic rather than mechanical. The target's state is unclear. The run has acted on it, and cannot tell what happened. The trace shows a sequence of observations that do not resolve into a picture — readings that could mean the change took effect, or could mean it did not, with nothing available to distinguish them.

This is its own particular misery, and it is one every engineer knows. A failure you can see is workable. A system that will not tell you what it is is a different kind of wall, because you cannot even establish what to try next. The run is not blocked by an obstacle. It is blocked by ambiguity.

Three cells, three targets that share no silhouette, three failures with nothing structurally in common: an impossible route, an absent dependency, an unreadable state. The caption underneath them has been earned rather than asserted.

And the grid is a compression, which the book says plainly. The investigators documented a large population working separate benchmark tasks in widely varying states; they did not preserve these nine particular views, side by side, at one instant. The simultaneity is analytic. What is being claimed is the shape of the population — many runs, many problems, no shared enemy — not that these nine screens existed together at a moment anyone could point to.
