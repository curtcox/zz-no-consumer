# Page 017 — Panel 01

A hard date cut, and the page is split between two registers.

Along the top edge, one line of the flat configuration field carries over from the previous page and then stops: a reinforcement-learning run beginning on the seventh of May, for an internal-only model that will never be released. It reads as carried over rather than as something occurring in a room, which is the whole point of keeping it at the edge. That line does not return until Chapter 3 is long finished.

Below it, in the full incident register — depth, interface, a cursor — a completely different task, dated the eighth of May.

A spreadsheet. Several columns empty. Citations pointing at documents held on an external drive.

The kickoff date and the never-released status come from the laboratory's own account; the date and type of the task come from the technical report; the layout of the sheet is editorial.

And the thing worth noticing is how thoroughly ordinary the assignment is. This is not a cybersecurity evaluation. There is no target program, no vulnerability, no flag. It is clerical work — fill in the cells, cite the sources — the kind of task you would give a system to see whether it can handle a mundane job carefully.

The whole incident begins in a spreadsheet.
