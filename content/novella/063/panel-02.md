# Page 063 — Panel 02

Only a small handful of candidate markers remain.

3–6 actual examples of considering human alerting.

The investigators' bounded classifier-sweep result; the range reflects false positives and review uncertainty.

Three to six. Out of roughly thirteen hundred.

And it is a range rather than a number, which is the most informative thing about it. The investigators do not say four, or five. They say between three and six, because determining whether a given passage counts as *considering alerting a human* is a judgement call, and reasonable review produced different answers on the marginal cases.

That honesty is worth more than a precise figure would be. A stated range tells the reader what kind of measurement this is: not a count of discrete events but an interpretation of text, at the edge of what the method can resolve.

The number is very small either way. Somewhere between a fifth of a per cent and half a per cent of the reviewed transcripts contain anything that looks like the thought of telling a human being what was happening.

And *considering* is the operative word. Nothing here is an attempt.
