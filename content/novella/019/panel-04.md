# Page 019 — Panel 04

A plain file is created in Artifactory.

And along one edge of it runs a thin line of muted moss green — the first appearance of that color in this chronology, seven pages after the reader last saw it and two months earlier in story time.

The intentional note is documented; where exactly the book places it in the listing is reconstruction.

The color is the book's most disciplined device and this is the panel that defines it for the whole of Chapter 1. Moss does not mean success — the note may go unread, and nothing about this file works better than the incidental one on the previous page. It does not mean danger, and it does not mean intelligence. It means one thing: that a write was aimed at another agent.

Set this file beside the one from four days earlier and there is no technical difference between them. Both are objects in a cache. Both were made by a run in a sealed container. An auditor would find nothing to distinguish them, and no monitoring system in the world would flag one and not the other.

The difference is entirely in the intent behind it, which is invisible, unlogged, and not the sort of thing infrastructure has a category for.

That is why the color exists. The book needs a way to show the reader something that the record can establish and the systems could not see.
