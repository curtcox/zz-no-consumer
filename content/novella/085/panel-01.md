# Page 085 — Panel 01

A split page. On the left, investigators querying an analysis model over private, raw transcripts, on premises. On the right, Curt querying a model over public reports, at home.

Both use a model to search, compress, and propose patterns.

The left method is documented; the right is dramatized; the comparison is the book's.

Three verbs, and they are the same three on both sides.

Search: find the relevant material in more of it than a person can read. Compress: reduce what is found to something reviewable. Propose patterns: suggest that these things go together and mean something.

That is what the analysis agents did across thirteen hundred transcripts, and it is what the model on the right does across two published reports.

The rhyme is real and the page states it first, before qualifying it, because a comparison that leads with its caveats is a comparison nobody has to take seriously.

The next three panels are the caveats, and they are as prominent as this panel is. The book's notes require it: the differences must be as visible as the resemblance.
