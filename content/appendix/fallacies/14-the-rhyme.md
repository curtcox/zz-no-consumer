## Where it appears

The book's central formal risk, and it names it on the page four times. Page 054:
`THESE ARE NOT THE SAME ACTORS. THEY DO NOT HAVE THE SAME DUTIES, KNOWLEDGE, OR MORAL
RESPONSIBILITY.` and `THE RHYME IS STRUCTURAL, NOT CAUSAL.` Page 085: `RESEMBLANCE DOES NOT
ERASE DIFFERENCE.` Page 087 carries it as a standing qualification. Page 098 opens on a
banner: `STRUCTURAL COMPARISON — NOT MORAL OR CAUSAL EQUIVALENCE`.

## Why the reasoning does not carry

The book intercuts an agent deciding to continue with an institution deciding to continue, and
the cut does the arguing. Panel adjacency is an argument in comics whether or not a caption
authorises it, and the inference it invites — that the two are the same kind of thing, and
that what is true of one transfers to the other — is not licensed by the resemblance.

The differences are not incidental. Humans have legal duties, moral agency, organisational
histories, the ability to communicate about the dilemma, and alternatives that a run inside an
evaluation does not have. An agent with an unmet objective and a laboratory with a competitor
share a payoff structure and nothing else, and the payoff structure is the smallest part of
what makes an institutional decision what it is.

The reason the book keeps the comparison is that the shared part is real and is the part that
generates the outcome: each actor wants an assurance it cannot create alone. The reason the
appendix records it as a fallacy risk anyway is that four disclaimers do not neutralise
fourteen pages of visual rhyme, and a reader is entitled to know the device is a device.
