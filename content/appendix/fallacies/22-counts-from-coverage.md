## Where it appears

Not on any page as a number. Pages [094](../../novella/06-everyone-continues/094.md) and [104](../../novella/06-everyone-continues/104.md) describe the open letter and the response
without stating a signatory count, and this entry records why the count is absent.

## Why the reasoning does not carry

Coverage of the collective cyber-defence letter has carried several different signatory
figures — more than 100, 116, and 128 have all appeared — and the figures cannot all be
current at once, because the signatory list is a live page that changes. A headline number is
a snapshot of a moving list, reported without the timestamp that would make it checkable.

The named source here is one specific dated headline, and it may be exactly right: "100 other
companies" is a floor claim and a floor claim is hard to falsify. The fallacy is not in that
headline; it is in the practice of citing any of these numbers as *the* count when the
signatory page is countable and free.

The same pattern applies to the report page counts. Coverage gives varying page counts for
both PDFs, and the documents themselves are the authority. This project excludes exact report
page counts copied from coverage for exactly this reason, and excludes an unmethodologised
$4–15M investigation-cost estimate on the same grounds.
