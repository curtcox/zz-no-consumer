## Where it appears

Not as a claim the book makes — the story contract states that "first," "second" and "third
civilization" are narrative analogies and must not imply consciousness, a unified polity,
clean generations, or a central controller. It appears as a framing the book inherited from
the most widely read analysis of the incident and had to work against, and pages 034, 109 and
111 are where it does that work.

## Why the reasoning does not carry

Three named populations with a rise-and-fall structure is a satisfying shape, and the record
does not have it.

The book's own population model says the "second" and "attack" populations are overlapping
views of one broad evaluation population, with runs entering and exiting continuously. Page
034 letters that there is no single clean founding event and at least two boards with more
than one mechanism. The 13 to 19 July branch is a different population on a single
organisation's account. And the wiki lane runs alongside the first, in the same weeks, with no
established relationship to any of them.

Once "civilization" is a countable noun, questions follow that the record cannot answer —
which one did a given agent belong to, when did the second end, what did the third inherit —
and the answers get supplied by the framing rather than by evidence.

To the author's credit, the piece carries post-publication corrections, and the framing has
been narrowed by its own author more than by its critics. That is why this entry names a
framing rather than an error of fact.
