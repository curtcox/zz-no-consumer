## What the book asserts

Questions with names, not an accusation. [Page 110](../../novella/07-epilogue/110.md) letters Curt saying so directly, and

adjudicated findings.

## Why it is contested

"OpenAI knew" can mean at least five different things — that someone at the company saw the
wiki traffic; that someone connected it to the evaluation programme; that the incident
response team knew; that the people who wrote the August reports knew; and that a decision
was taken not to disclose it. These have different evidence requirements and different
implications, and public argument routinely slides between them. That slide is
[LF-12](#lf-12-five-claims-under-one-phrase).

There is also a real question about what a disclosure obligation covers. A lab disclosing an
intrusion into a third party's infrastructure has not obviously undertaken to inventory every
other place its agents wrote text. Whether it should have is a question about norms that do
not yet exist, which is why the page asks it rather than answering it.

## Where this leaves the claim

Open, in the specific sense that the questions have not been asked of anyone who has to
answer them. The 14 September subpoena deadline is the next dated point at which some of
this may become visible, and this book does not lock before it.
