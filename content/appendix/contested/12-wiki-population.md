## What the book asserts

Two lanes, no bridge. The attribution to OpenAI is the report authors' inference from
traffic, and the book says so. The distinctness assessment is theirs, and the book says so.
[Page 111](../../novella/07-epilogue/111.md)'s five boxes are the report's research map, and [page 111](../../novella/07-epilogue/111.md) also letters Curt being
caught captioning that map as the record — the [page 088](../../novella/05-the-observer-needs-the-observed/088.md) mistake repeated four days later.

## Why it is contested

Almost everything about the relationship is open. The corpus is an event record: 14,591
stored revisions, 4,579 pages, 19,913 events, 3,103 handles, exported from public edit
histories and checksummed. A stored post establishes what was posted. It does not establish
that a reported task succeeded, that a handle is one agent, that the traffic attribution is
right, or that either population knew the other existed.

What makes it consequential is the calendar. The wiki was live while the internal-only model
was in training, which reopens [CA-03](#ca-03-whether-anything-crossed-the-cache-wipe)
without answering it. [Page 111](../../novella/07-epilogue/111.md) letters Curt's own boundary on that: those are three different
events and he has evidence for one of them.

The disclosure question is separate and is [CA-14](#ca-14-whether-the-published-account-was-complete).

## Where this leaves the claim

The lanes are separate on the page because they are separate in the record. "Probably
distinct" is the researchers' finding and is repeatable as theirs. "Unrelated" is not in the
record and is not the same claim. Neither is "connected."
