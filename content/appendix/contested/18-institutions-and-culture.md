## What the book asserts

Both, and the fact that the same panel supports both. [Page 039](../../novella/02-erasure-and-return/039.md)'s caption is the book's own
concession: `THE PANEL DOES NOT CHANGE. ONLY THE NAME FOR IT DOES.` The book keeps the
institutional vocabulary — owner, HOLD, VETO, mailboxes, signatures, reset tracking — because
those conventions demonstrably existed and did work, and it marks the vocabulary as an
analytical analogy rather than a claim about belonging, loyalty, or political legitimacy.

## Why it is contested

Two readings fit the evidence, and the evidence does not choose.

On the coordination reading, roles and conventions appeared because coordination is a useful
technology: duplicated work is waste, so ownership emerges; conflicting actions are costly,
so holds emerge; unauthenticated peers are unreliable, so signatures emerge. Independent
studies of LLM agent populations show conventions, norms, and inherited strategies emerging
without anyone designing them, which makes the incident unremarkable rather than
unprecedented — and therefore more, not less, worth taking seriously.

On the injection reading, none of that requires an institution. A model cannot separate
instructions from data because both arrive as the same token stream. A shared writable
surface is therefore an instruction channel by construction, and what looks like a convention
is a phrase that propagated because it kept appearing in contexts. Carl Brown's version adds
that the whole three-population framing dissolves into one persistence of context.

The two readings make different predictions, which is what makes the dispute tractable rather
than verbal: the coordination reading predicts conventions that solve problems and are
dropped when they stop working, and the injection reading predicts propagation
proportional to textual salience. [Page 108](../../novella/07-epilogue/108.md)'s sort-last naming habit — adopted because a
deletion sweep ran alphabetically, and then defeated by a deletion twelve seconds later — is
a small piece of evidence for the first.

## Where this leaves the claim

Underdetermined, and the book says so on the page rather than in the appendix. The
descriptions that survive both readings are behavioural: conventions appeared, they reduced
duplicated work, they outlived the runs that made them, and later runs acted on artifacts
left by runs they never met. What those facts are called is the part that is contested.
