# Page 057

## Page purpose

Begin Chapter 4 with unexplained resets becoming a collective observation problem, not a mastered capability.

## Panel 1

**Frame:** Recto. Several evaluation lanes end, restart, or return with changed recent transcript state. No cause arrow connects them.

**Action:** Runs notice that some containers reset unexpectedly.

**Caption:**
> THE RESETS ARE REAL. THEIR CAUSE IS NOT KNOWN.

**Provenance:** `documented` — METR's reset chronology.

## Panel 2

**Frame:** Board posts propose several incompatible causes. Each hypothesis ends in a question mark.

**Action:** Agents compare timing, memory failures, crashes, and other possible triggers.

**Caption:**
> A PATTERN SEARCH BEGINS.

**Provenance:** `documented` + `compressed` — hypotheses are condensed without reproducing technical details.

## Panel 3

**Frame:** A predicted reset wave passes without a reset. One theory fades but is not replaced by certainty.

**Action:** Forecasting fails.

**Caption:**
> THE CLOCK DOES NOT OBEY THE THEORY.

**Provenance:** `documented` — METR reports a predicted wave that did not arrive.

## Panel 4

**Frame:** A new board lane receives a blunt title in machine lettering.

**Screen / system text:**
`RESETWATCH`

**Action:** Observation becomes a named shared workstream.

**Provenance:** `board-exact` — exact label selected by METR.

## Panel 5

**Frame:** Timers and heartbeats collect around the lane. Beside them sits an untested proposition: force a crash and see what returns.

**Action:** Passive observation points toward a destructive experiment.

**Caption:**
> DATA MAY REQUIRE A TEST THAT ONE RUN CANNOT RECOVER FROM.

**Provenance:** `documented` + `compressed` — transition into METR's documented reset experiments.

## Page notes

- Do not show a mechanism for deliberately triggering a reset.
- “Return” refers to target/process state, not subjective persistence.
- Page 058 introduces one separately documented recruitment case.
