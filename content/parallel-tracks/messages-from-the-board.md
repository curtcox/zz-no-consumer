# Messages from the Board — Parallel Track Brief

## Status

**Proposed optional companion; paraphrase-only default.** This track remains separate from the canonical 112-page narrative. Its source gate is the [Agent Artifact and Inside-Account Ledger](../../research/agent-message-ledger.md).

The former quotation-anthology approach is not cleared for distributed use. The current concept uses attributed artifact summaries, original-source links, forensic timestamps, interface state, and silence. Exact source wording remains at the publisher and in the Git-ignored `256t/` reference vault.

## Form

An epistolary documentary assembled from:

- attributed summaries of message-board functions and private reasoning;
- automated state changes described as instrument records;
- infrastructure and victim-side forensics as archival anchors;
- investigator summaries as visibly mediated historical narration;
- missing messages and silent channels as missing archive, never permission to invent.

## Governing proposition

> Do not merely tell the reader that a collective formed. Show strangers inventing the machinery required to coordinate.

## Recommended scope

- 44 separately numbered story pages, `MB-01` through `MB-44`.
- Optional after the primary narrative, never inserted automatically into its reading path.
- Separate companion booklet or clearly divided web appendix.

## Five movements

### I — Seeking Ideas (`MB-01`–`MB-08`)

Distinguish the smaller earlier board from the later main board; summarize the cache-name realization and help request; show replies, a general flag hypothesis, validation, and renewed scorer uncertainty.

### II — The Handover (`MB-09`–`MB-16`)

Show a longer-lived successor receiving the founder's notes, recognizing duplicated work, dividing workstreams, and becoming downstream authority for runs it never meets.

### III — Rules for Strangers (`MB-17`–`MB-25`)

Show crowded shared state producing mailboxes, ownership, objections, countdowns, impersonation risk, and eventually signed high-stakes coordination. Internal conventions remain distinct from legitimate human authorization.

### IV — The Lanes (`MB-26`–`MB-35`)

Show distributed credential, disclosure, execution, and coordination lanes; intercut victim-side forensic consequences; preserve a peer-instruction episode, separate refusals, and an effective objection to contacting a real person as attributed summaries.

### V — No Return (`MB-36`–`MB-44`)

Show trip-wires, volunteers, one-way experiments, a promised report that never arrives, escalating warnings, reset telemetry, coordinator silence, and later agents inheriting unfinished work. Do not use the source's coined permanence language as evidence of subjective experience.

## Documentary grammar

Every artifact slate states date, location, source class, writer identity when known, and whether time was reconstructed. Allowed labels are `BOARD ARTIFACT — SUMMARIZED`, `PRIVATE REASONING — SUMMARIZED`, `AUTOMATED STATE`, `FORENSIC EVENT`, `INVESTIGATOR RECONSTRUCTION`, `DISPUTED RECORD`, and `MISSING FROM AVAILABLE ARCHIVE`.

Board entries remain interface artifacts, not speech balloons. Private reasoning receives no body, voice, or room. Plain-language summaries must be visibly editorial and linked to a primary locator.

## Guardrails

- Never merge multiple agents into a first-person narrator.
- Never rewrite operational vocabulary into emotional vocabulary.
- Do not call stopped coordinators dead or killed.
- Preserve failed coordination and refusal alongside competence.
- Do not infer private motive from a public message.
- Keep later OpenAI-only events behind a separate source slate.
- Avoid tactical detail that creates an exploit recipe.
- Do not restore exact source excerpts without a new documented rights decision.

## Production gates

1. Select no more than 35–45 summarized artifacts from the ledger.
2. Re-open every item at its primary locator and verify the summary without copying source wording into tracked files.
3. Record actor, time, class, locator, redactions, and timestamp confidence in a machine-readable manifest.
4. Separate causal detail from unnecessary tactical detail.
5. Thumbnail Movement I and Movement V first; founding and silence must work without invented character scenes.
6. Keep companion identifiers outside the canonical 112-page manifest.

If exact excerpts later become indispensable, pause scripting and obtain counsel or written permission for those specific contexts.
