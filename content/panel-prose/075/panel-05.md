# Page 075 — Panel 05

Three human investigators stand before the evidence field. A six-day clock occupies one corner.

The available human review window is six days.

The investigators and their collaborators report six on-premises days across three visits.

Three people. Six days. Thirteen hundred transcripts of millions of tokens each, plus twenty million namespace entries.

The arithmetic does not need stating and the panel states it by composition: the humans are small in the frame and the evidence field is not.

And the constraint is not laziness or corner-cutting. This material was confidential. Reviewing it meant being physically present, on premises, under an agreed scope, for whatever days could be arranged. That is what independent review of somebody else's incident data actually looks like — negotiated access, in a room, with a clock.

Six days is a serious commitment. It is also nothing at all against this corpus.

The book has now laid out the two halves of the chapter's problem: an enormous body of evidence, and the actual quantity of human attention available to read it.

The next panel says what follows from putting those side by side.
