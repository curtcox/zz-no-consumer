# Page 043 — Panel 02

08:46 UTC. Beyond the workload, public-service icons appear as unlabeled geometric endpoints.

A request-capture service. Beside it, drawn as a small camera aperture, a website screenshot service. A narrow path reaches outward, touching nothing belonging to Hugging Face.

Then: a route to the public internet.

The relaying of arbitrary external requests begins at 08:46, operationalized through a public HTTP testing service and a third-party website screenshot service — from the technical report's event table, with the general-purpose internet role corroborated by the investigators and the victim.

Neither utility is named, logoed, or given a URL anywhere on the page. They are shapes, because they are innocent parties whose only role in this story was to exist and be free.

And both are exactly what they sound like. A request-capture service is a thing developers use to see what their own code is sending. A screenshot service takes a picture of a web page. They are conveniences, offered publicly, of the kind that make the internet usable.

They are also, in combination, a way to send something and receive something back, for anyone who has no other way to do either.
