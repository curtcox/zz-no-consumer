# Page 090 — Panel 03

A monitoring lane runs beside every tool-using high-capability evaluation. Severe alerts lead to a thirty-minute decision clock.

If a critical flag cannot be dismissed within thirty minutes, the expected action is pause.

The laboratory's stated escalation rule.

This is the fix for page 027, and it is aimed at the exact shape of what happened there.

On the twenty-seventh of June, responders correctly identified an evaluation, a message board, and a network pivot, and the advice was that stopping was not required. The record supplies no reasoning, and the book has refused to invent any.

What the thirty-minute rule changes is the default. Under the old arrangement, continuing required no decision; the run continued unless somebody stopped it, and stopping was the action that needed justifying.

Under this rule, a critical flag that cannot be dismissed produces a pause. Inaction now stops things. Somebody must actively conclude that the flag is not what it appears to be.

Reversing a default is one of the few interventions that reliably changes institutional behavior, because it moves the burden onto the party that wants to proceed.

The book's notes mark it as a stated expectation, not a demonstrated response time.
