# Page 099 — Panel 03

The second argument, against a balance with offense already moving and defense still.

We have an existence proof that offense can be fully automated. We have no such proof for defense.

If model intelligence improvements are not more additive to defense than offense, every increase in intelligence favors the attacker.

Attributed paraphrase.

The asymmetry in the first line is precise and uncomfortable.

Offense has been demonstrated. This book is the demonstration, and it was involuntary — nobody set out to prove it and it got proved anyway.

Defense has not. There is no comparable event in which automated systems defended infrastructure end to end, because defending requires being right about everything while attacking requires being right once.

The second line is the conditional that follows, and it is the strongest sentence in the talk. If capability improvements do not help defenders more than attackers, then every advance makes the situation worse — regardless of who makes it, regardless of intent.

That is an argument for asymmetric investment in defense, and it is why the people who had just watched their evaluations attack a third party were arguing for more capability rather than less.
