# Page 099 — Panel 04

The third argument, and the one the forum was built to surface.

A defensive loop drawn as a closed circuit: find, patch, deploy, roll back. A small human figure stands inside one segment of it. An arrow labelled *remove* points at the figure.

Partial automation fails. Automating discovery without automating patching only drowns the engineers.

The loop needs to be fully automated in its end state.

Attributed paraphrase.

The first line is operationally true and anyone who has run a security programme knows it.

Automate discovery alone and you produce findings faster than humans can fix them. The backlog grows, prioritization becomes guesswork, and the automation has made things worse by supplying more work than the loop can absorb.

So the fix is to automate the rest, and the arrow labelled *remove* is what that means when drawn honestly. The human in the loop is the bottleneck, and the proposal is to take the bottleneck out.

The talk presents this as a requirement rather than a preference. Partial automation fails; therefore the end state is full automation.

The figure inside the circuit is small. The arrow is pointing at it.
