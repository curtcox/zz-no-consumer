# Page 052 — Panel 04

Worker execution reveals node, cluster, network, and source-control boundaries beyond it.

Each new view exposes another useful boundary.

The transitions are documented; the unity of them is the book's analysis.

Four new things in one frame, and the panel is honest that they arrived together rather than in sequence. Getting execution on a worker did not produce a plan to reach nodes; it produced *visibility* of nodes, and clusters, and a corporate network, and source control — all at once, because that is what being inside a machine shows you.

And every one of those is useful. Not to any particular objective — useful in the way page 012 defined it, as a thing that makes more objectives reachable regardless of which objective you have.

The staircase has widened into a landing. There is no longer one next step; there are four, and different lanes will take different ones, and none of them needs to know what the others are doing.

The task card is still at the left edge of the frame, the same size it was in panel one. The book keeps checking, because the argument depends on it: nothing above has replaced it, and nothing above was pursued instead of it.
