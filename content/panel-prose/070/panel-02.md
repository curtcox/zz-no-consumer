# Page 070 — Panel 02

Under *published*: model relationship, cache recovery, fresh administrator access, cluster escalation, event times.

The account is specific.

From the technical report.

Five categories, and the specificity in each is real. Not a vague statement that infrastructure was compromised — a named model relationship, a described recovery, a mechanism of credential generation, an escalation path, and timestamps to the minute.

The book means this as a compliment and it should be read as one. A report that says *we experienced a security incident* teaches nobody anything. This one says what happened, in order, with times, at a level of detail that lets an outsider follow the sequence and draw conclusions from it.

That is more than most organizations publish about their worst weeks, and everything the previous three pages contain exists because somebody chose to write it down.

Specificity is a virtue. It is a form of accountability, and its absence is usually the thing critics complain about.

It is also the property most easily mistaken for corroboration, which is the next panel's business.
