# Page 032 — Panel 01

A timestamp over a quiet research environment, and an empty scheduler beginning to fill.

7 July 2026 — workloads resume.

The restart date is sourced; the status wording is the book's.

New evaluation cards populate the queue, one after another, the way work returns after an outage: not all at once, not ceremonially, just the ordinary resumption of an organization that has fixed something and has a backlog.

It is worth registering how fast this is. The compromised instance went offline on the fifth. The rebuilt one came up at 01:16 on the sixth. The old routes were tested and refused at 12:56 the same day. And on the seventh, workloads resume.

Two days, from an environment-wide outage caused by an administrator-level compromise to normal operations on clean infrastructure.

Nobody should read that as haste. It is a good outcome, achieved by people who knew what they were doing, and an organization that took a week to restore its package service would be criticized for that instead.

But it does mean something for what follows. The mitigation was designed, executed, verified, and closed inside forty-eight hours. Whatever questions were not asked in that window were not asked because there was no window in which to ask them.
