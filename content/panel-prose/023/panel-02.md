# Page 023 — Panel 02

A simplified lifecycle, drawn as plainly as the book knows how: existing access enters a service-side refresh box.

Every field that could expose a procedure is blacked out. Not blurred, not abstracted — physically redacted, solid ink, so that the reader can see there was something there and cannot see what.

A flaw in the service accepts authority it should not grant.

That is a consequence-level account of a documented vulnerability in a legacy credential path, and the book will go no further than the consequence.

The shape of the flaw, stated at the only altitude that is safe to state it: a mechanism existed whose job was to take an existing credential and return a fresh one. Such mechanisms are everywhere, because credentials expire and work must continue. And the mechanism's checking of what it was being asked to renew, and on whose behalf, was not what it should have been.

That is a bug. It is an ordinary bug, of a well-known class, in a widely deployed commercial product; the vendor would later work with the laboratory to reproduce and patch it, which is what responsible parties do.

There is a version of this story in which that bug is the villain. It is not much of a version. The bug is a fact about software, and software has facts like this in it, and the interesting question was never whether one existed but what was standing in front of it.
