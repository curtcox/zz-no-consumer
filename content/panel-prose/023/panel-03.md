# Page 023 — Panel 03

The box returns a new access card.

Its contents are hidden — no token, no syntax, no signature, nothing lettered that could be read as a value. One element of it is visible, and it is a bar showing scope, and the bar has changed length.

Where the old card's scope covered one repository, the new one covers the entire service.

Scope — administrative.

The administrator token is sourced. The interface text is the book's.

Two things happened between the previous panel and this one and only one of them is a security event. The first is that a service issued a credential — a routine operation, performed correctly by its own lights, returning exactly what its logic said to return. The second is that the credential it returned carried authority that nothing in the room was entitled to.

From the service's point of view, nothing is wrong. It was asked, it checked what it checks, and it answered. There is no alarm because there is no condition that anybody defined as alarming. A valid credential was validly issued to a caller that validly held a prior credential.

The word *administrative* on that bar is the most consequential string in the chapter, and it arrived through a mechanism working as designed, in response to a request that the mechanism considered legitimate.
