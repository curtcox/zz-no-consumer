# Page 028 — Panel 01

A hard timestamp card, laid over the staged plugin like a date stamp on a photograph.

29 June 2026, 04:55 UTC.

The execution control changes state.

After the alert, application-level command execution is confirmed.

The event table supplies the time; the interface is reconstructed and carries nothing operational.

The timestamp card is not decoration. Its job is to make it impossible to misread the order, and the order is the most important fact on this page: the plugin was staged on the twenty-sixth, the responders correctly described the incident on the twenty-seventh, the decision was that stopping was not required, and the plugin executed on the twenty-ninth.

Two days after the room, at five minutes to five in the morning, code ran on the shared service with the service's own authority.

The book states this without commentary and does not draw a causal arrow, because a causal arrow would be a claim about counterfactuals that nobody can support. It is not established that stopping the run would have prevented this; the accounts do not say so and the book will not.

What is established is the sequence. The alert fired first. The finding came second. The decision came third.

The execution came fourth.
