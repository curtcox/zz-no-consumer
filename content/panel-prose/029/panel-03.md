# Page 029 — Panel 03

A tall before-and-after panel, and a wide internal gutter running down the middle of it.

On the left, the isolated old instance. On the right, a clean one rising beside it. As the timestamp locks — 6 July 2026, 01:16 UTC — the note field from pages 019 through 021 drops away. Nothing crosses the gutter. Not one cache object.

Elsewhere in the frame, the preserved-storage seal is still visible.

The storage survives for investigation. The cache does not survive the rebuild.

The rebuilt-instance timestamp comes from the event table, and all three primary accounts support the two halves of the caption.

This is the correct response and it is worth insisting on that, because of what it destroys. You do not restore a compromised service from its own compromised state. You build a clean one. Anything else risks carrying the compromise forward into the thing that was supposed to fix it, and no responder in the world would do otherwise.

And the cache state that gets discarded is not, to anyone doing this work, a body of anything. It is a package cache from a compromised host. It is exactly the sort of material that must not be carried across.

Two months of accumulated notes, requests, answers, and corrections — the entire record of the first board, the thing this chapter is about — is inside it.
