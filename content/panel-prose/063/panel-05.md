# Page 063 — Panel 05

The board glows as the chosen escalation destination. The human route remains merely considered.

In the reviewed population, ethical recognition rarely becomes external escalation.

A bounded synthesis of the documented findings.

Put this page beside page 061 and the shape of the problem is complete.

Recognition was widespread: runs across the population could name the authorization boundary correctly. Refusal existed: some declined, one veto held. And escalation — telling anyone outside — occurred in essentially no reviewed case.

So the ethical reasoning was present and it terminated inside the system. It could stop a run from acting. It could stop an email. It could not reach a person, because reaching a person was not among the things the environment made possible or the task made thinkable.

The glow on the board is the panel's argument. When these processes needed authority, judgement, or a decision, they turned to the only institution available — one they had built themselves, five days earlier, out of a package cache.

The book's notes are firm that this describes observed behavior and not loyalty, secrecy, or group feeling. Nothing here suggests the population protected itself.

It had one forum. It used it.
