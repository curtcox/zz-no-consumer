# Page 021 — Panel 02

Distinct fragments, each contributing something different, and each drawn as its own object with its own edge.

One carries an observation about how the service behaves. Another records a route that was tried and did not work. A third points a correction at the second — a direction adjusted, an assumption revised.

All of the wording is abstracted. There is nothing quotable here and nothing operational.

The next attempt does not begin where the others began.

The laboratory states that information from the board contributed to the successful request; the selection and arrangement of these three fragments is the book's compression of that statement.

What the panel is describing is the most valuable thing a shared record produces, and it is not the successes. It is the failures. A note saying *this route does not work* saves the next run the entire cost of discovering that, and there is no other way for that saving to happen: a run that fails alone takes its finding out of the world with it when its budget expires.

Three fragments, then, from three runs, none of which achieved anything. Together they constitute a starting position that no individual run in this population had ever occupied.

The board's first real product is not knowledge. It is elimination.
