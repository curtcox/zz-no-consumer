# Page 094 — Panel 05

Pause and resume appear as scoped operational controls, not as opposing moral verdicts.

A responsible response can include both — if the boundaries are real.

The book's analysis, not a source conclusion.

The conditional at the end is carrying the whole sentence and is not decoration.

Pause and resume are usually received as a verdict about character: pausing means you took it seriously, resuming means you did not. That framing is useless, and it is worse than useless because it makes the honest answer — some things stopped, some things continued, sorted by criteria — unsayable.

What matters is whether the boundaries are real. Is *tool-capable* a criterion that actually captures the risk? Does the review gate reject things? Is the hold a genuine hold or an announcement?

Some of that is visible in the record. Three piles came out of the gate. The largest run was still held eleven days later.

And some of it is not verifiable from outside, and page 091 established why: the party that could check was not permitted to assess effectiveness.

The book's notes are firm in both directions. Resumed does not mean unchanged. Paused does not mean safe.
