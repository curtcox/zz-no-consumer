# Page 085 — Panel 03

Left: an independent-investigation mandate, and scoped factual questions to answer.

Right: an adaptation brief, and a thesis it wants to test.

One answers scoped questions under a mandate. One is testing a thesis it already has.

The roles, stated accurately.

This difference is worse than the access difference, and the book puts it second because it is the one a reader is least likely to supply on their own.

A mandate is a constraint. The investigators were commissioned to answer particular questions, under an agreed scope, and their published findings are accountable to that. What they wanted to be true is not supposed to determine what they report, and the structure exists to make that hard.

A thesis is the opposite. The creator came to this material with concerns already written on a legal pad — page 015 showed the three words — and chose this incident because it touched them.

Page 087 will make that disclosure in full and at length. Here it is stated as a structural fact about the two pipelines: one is designed to resist the analyst's preferences, and the other is powered by them.

The instruments are similar. What surrounds them is not.
