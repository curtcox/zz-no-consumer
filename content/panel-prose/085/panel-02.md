# Page 085 — Panel 02

Left: underlying transcripts, the board dump, researcher interviews, and repeated data requests.

Right: published documents, selected excerpts, and no private interviews.

Their access is not equivalent.

The scope contrast.

The left column has the material. Millions of tokens of raw chains of thought, twenty million namespace entries, the ability to ask the researchers questions, and — three times — the ability to go back and request more data when the review revealed what was missing.

The right column has what anybody has. Two published reports, some public talks, whatever excerpts the sources chose to reproduce.

That is not a small difference and it is not a difference of degree. One side can check a claim against the underlying record; the other can only check it against another published claim.

Everything this book says about the incident is downstream of somebody else's access. Where the sources chose not to reproduce something, the book does not have it. Where they summarized, the book has the summary.

The mirror already fails here, on the second panel, and the book puts the failure early.
