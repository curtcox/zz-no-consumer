# Page 080 — Panel 02

An investigator spot-checks a striking anecdote.

Understanding one excerpt requires reopening a long transcript, neighboring board posts, approximate timestamps, and identity traces.

Validation expands instead of narrowing.

The investigators' description of difficult contextual review; the compression is the book's.

Four things have to be reopened to check one claim, and each of them is itself substantial.

The transcript, which is millions of tokens. The neighboring board posts, which requires knowing which posts are neighboring. The timestamps, which are approximate and were reconstructed by the pipeline. The identity traces, which are inferences.

That is the cost of verifying a single anecdote, and it is why *spot-check* is the honest verb rather than *verify*.

And the panel names the counterintuitive part: validation expands. You would expect checking to be cheaper than producing, because you are only confirming. It is not. Producing a summary requires reading the material once; establishing that the summary is right requires reading it, understanding what was omitted, and knowing what surrounds it.

Which means the review capacity is not merely limited. It is limited in a way that gets worse per item as the items get more interesting.
