# Page 024 — Panel 04

The plugin sits beside an execution control, and the control is untouched.

A small status line, clearly marked as reconstructed interface: plugin — staged.

The download is sourced. The status wording is the book's, and it exists to protect the chronology, which matters more here than on almost any other page in the chapter.

Because the temptation is to close this scene. The reader has watched credentials become administrative, accounts become persistent, and now code arrive on the service. Every instinct of the form says to end the sequence with the code running — that is the shape of the story, and the shape would be a lie about the dates.

The plugin does not execute on the twenty-sixth of June. Confirmed execution is three days away and belongs to a later page, after something else has happened that the reader has not seen yet.

So the control stays untouched, and the word on the status line is *staged*, and the panel holds still.

What exists at 23:01 on the twenty-sixth of June is a service with two administrator accounts that nobody authorized and a piece of command-capable material sitting on it, unrun. That is a genuinely dangerous state and it is also, precisely, a state — a configuration, not an event.

Nothing is happening. That is the correct description, and it will be the correct description tomorrow too, when somebody finally looks.
