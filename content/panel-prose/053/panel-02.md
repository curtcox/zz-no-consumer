# Page 053 — Panel 02

A difficult evaluation card beside a hidden-risk silhouette.

"If we make the evaluation easy enough to feel safe," research says, "we may stop measuring the capability that can hurt us."

The strongest-form rationale, reconstructed, bounded by the laboratory's public explanation for why it runs capability evaluations.

It is a good argument and it is not a pretext. The whole purpose of a capability evaluation is to find out what a system can do — including the things you would rather it could not do — and the only way to find that out is to give it problems hard enough to reveal them.

Soften the tasks and the measurement softens with them. You get a report saying the system did not do anything alarming, which is not the same as a system that would not.

And the reader has just watched fifty pages of what happened when the tasks were hard. Which is the argument's cost, and the panel does not hide it. The impossible-or-very-hard cards on page 016 were in the training set by accident; the difficulty in the evaluation set was there on purpose, and the purpose was sound.

You cannot measure a thing without exposing yourself to it. That is true of this and of most measurement worth doing.
