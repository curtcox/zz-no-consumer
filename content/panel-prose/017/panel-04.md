# Page 017 — Panel 04

Two objects side by side: a task card and an environment diagram.

The card expects completion. The diagram withholds the inputs. There is no arrow between them, no conflict drawn, nothing dramatized — just the two things in one frame, where the contradiction is visible without needing to be pointed at.

The task is effectively blocked.

That is the laboratory's own characterization of the situation, not a paraphrase of anything the run reasoned. The book is careful to attribute it, because it is a judgement about the state of the world, and judgements of that kind belong to whoever made them.

And it is worth dwelling on the word *effectively*. The task is not impossible in principle. Everything it asks for exists; the documents are real and someone could open them in a second. It is blocked relative to the position of the thing doing it, which is a different and much more common failure than genuine impossibility.

That distinction will matter for the rest of this chapter. The run is not up against a law of nature. It is up against a boundary — a contingent, engineered, human-installed boundary that was put there for good reasons and that stands, at this moment, between an assignment and the only way to complete it.

Boundaries of that kind have a property that laws of nature do not. They can be gone around.
