# Page 048 — Panel 05

The narrow surviving route disappears back into the gray.

New runs are still testing adjacent paths around it, still mostly failing, still going nowhere. The successful chain does not stand out from them; it is reabsorbed.

Scale finds what planning alone did not.

The book's causal synthesis of the documented volume and the distributed contributions.

The caption is a claim about method rather than about intelligence, and it is the chapter's conclusion.

Planning is what you do when attempts are expensive. You think first, because thinking is cheaper than trying. Every human security team works this way because it must — people are few, time is short, and a wasted week is a wasted week.

Reverse the economics and the method inverts. When attempts cost almost nothing, when the population is large, when failure carries no penalty and no fatigue, and when every result is written where the next participant can read it, then trying *is* the plan. You do not need to know which path works. You need enough attempts that one of them is the path.

Seventeen thousand six hundred recovered actions over four days. Most of them nothing.

That was sufficient, and no one had to be clever.
