# Page 039 — Panel 01

The dotted arrow is still on the pad, still pointing at the underlined words.

Beside the model's window a second window has opened. It carries a different typeface, a byline, and a date: Carl Brown — Internet of Bugs — 3 September 2026.

Both are text on screens. Neither has a face.

One of these windows is a person.

The scene is invented; the critique is a real dated publication, registered in the source index and admitted to the creator register under the contract's rules. What is not invented is the formal fact the caption points at.

The reader cannot tell the two windows apart by looking. Same brightness, same size, same rectangular block of text arriving in the same place on the same desk. One of them is a large language model producing a response. The other is a named human being who published an argument on a stated date and can be read at the link.

The book has kept the model faceless since page 013 for reasons of honesty — it has no body and drawing one would lie. The consequence, arrived at here, is that a person appearing by the same route is equally faceless, and the two are indistinguishable at the level of the artifact.

This is not a metaphor. It is a description of what a screen is.
