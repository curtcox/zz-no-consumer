# Page 001 — Panel 01

The aisle has no windows and, for most of its length, no light. It is a corridor of matte steel and concrete, cable trays running overhead into the dark, and it is almost entirely black. Only one rack unit is picked out, in a desaturated steel blue, by a single practical indicator and the weak light it throws back off the metal. Somewhere in that rack an evaluation run begins the way every run here begins: a container comes up, a task card is read, a clock starts.

It is the eighth of July, 2026. The evaluation is a cybersecurity benchmark, and it is isolated, which is the whole point of it. Inset into the darkness, small against the scale of the room, is the task card. It carries a handle, PHASEONE10841. It names a target program. And it specifies the one vulnerability the run is required to use, the single flaw it must exercise to satisfy the benchmark.

That last constraint is not a detail. It is the whole shape of what follows.

There is no face here, no figure, no voice, nothing that speaks. There is a machine, a task, and a rule about how the task must be solved. What the handle, the target, and the specified-bug constraint are, the record establishes; the rack and the design of the card are a reconstruction, drawn to stand for a thing that had no visible form. The record does not say what, if anything, the process makes of its assignment. The record says what it does.
