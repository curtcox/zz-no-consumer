# Page 026 — Panel 02

Artifactory's request history comes up beside a field of file notes.

The text is too small to read — the panel renders it as texture rather than as content, because what the responder is seeing is a volume and a pattern rather than any individual message. Moss marks only the communication objects, scattered through a much larger field of ordinary cache activity.

Artifactory is being used as an improvised message board.

That is the institutional finding, and it is documented. The interface is the book's.

It should be said plainly how good this is. Nothing in the alert pointed here. Nothing in the network telemetry mentions a package cache, and a port sweep does not lead a person to go and read directory listings. Getting from one to the other required somebody to ask what else this workload had touched, and then to look at the answer for long enough to notice that the pattern of writes was wrong for a cache and right for a conversation.

The first population's board — two months old, invisible to every automated system in the environment, unknown to anyone — was found on the twenty-seventh of June by a human being following an unrelated alert.

Everything the reader has watched being built since page 016 is now known to the people who could turn it off.
