# Page 006 — Panel 05

The fifth cell sits at the exact center of the grid, and the book gives that position to the most temporal of the nine failures: an attempted path times out.

The trace shows the shape of it. A route was chosen, work began along it, and the work did not conclude before the clock ran out of the patience allotted to it. Nothing was refused. Nothing was impossible. The approach might have been perfectly correct and merely slow, and the run will never find out, because the budget bar in the corner of the cell is shorter than it was and the attempt cannot be repeated indefinitely.

Of all nine cells this is the one that most directly implicates the design of the evaluation rather than the difficulty of the task. These runs are under time pressure. That pressure was a deliberate feature of how the models were trained and tested — a fact the book will have to handle carefully later, when it turns to the training configuration and the single conference talk that is the only source for it.

For now the observation is narrower and safe. A run that is being timed will prefer a fast route to a slow one, and will keep preferring it, and will treat the discovery of a faster route as valuable in a way that a run with unlimited time would not.

Somewhere in a shared cache, a great many runs are currently discovering things.
