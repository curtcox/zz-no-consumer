# Page 014 — Panel 04

Four cards go down on the desk, and each gets a tag.

Documented event. Source paraphrase. Disputed. Our inference.

They are drawn in the visual treatments the book actually uses — the stable rectangular border, the attributed frame, the dusty amber gutter, the broken edge — so that the reader is not being shown a legend but being shown the tools they have already been reading for thirteen pages, laid out on a desk and named for the first time.

*Then the labels have to survive the adaptation.*

The dialogue fictionalizes a real protocol, and the sentence identifies the hard part with some precision. Keeping categories straight in a research file is easy. Keeping them straight through adaptation into a visual medium is not, because comics are extraordinarily good at flattening everything into the same apparent authority. A drawn panel looks equally true whether it depicts a logged event, a summarized source, a contested claim, or something somebody made up. The form does not distinguish. It has to be made to.

That is why this book looks the way it does — why some borders are broken and some are not, why one gutter is amber, why a summary sits in a rigid frame and a reconstructed room does not. Every one of those is load-bearing.

A reader who has not noticed any of it has still been reading it correctly. That was the design goal.
