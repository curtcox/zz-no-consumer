# Page 087 — Panel 05

Two labels share the desk. Thesis shapes selection. Evidence can resist thesis.

Neither wins the frame.

A starting bias does not make every claim false. Hiding it would make the claims harder to evaluate.

An epistemic framing.

Both labels are true and the composition refuses to rank them.

*Thesis shapes selection* is the concession, made fully: what is in this book was chosen by somebody with prior commitments, and a different person would have made a different book from the same material.

*Evidence can resist thesis* is the reason the enterprise is not futile. The record pushed back repeatedly and the pushing is documented — page 039 broke the chapter's central argument, page 040 narrowed what survived, page 052 put the convergence argument's strongest counter-evidence directly above its thesis, page 073 struck out the line the material most wanted.

None of that would have happened if the thesis simply determined the output.

And the caption's second line is the whole defence available to any work of this kind. The bias cannot be removed. It can be declared, which converts an invisible influence into something a reader can weigh.

Disclosure is not absolution. The book's own notes say so.
