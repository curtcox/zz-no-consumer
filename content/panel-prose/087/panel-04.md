# Page 087 — Panel 04

Curt moves the complicating facts into the page plan rather than discarding them. Earlier chapter guardrails and counterexamples light up.

"He's describing them. He's also describing me. I don't have their incentive. I have a thesis, which is worse, because it doesn't show up on a balance sheet.

"The fit is a reason to investigate harder, not permission to simplify."

The creator's application of the critic's point to himself is his own, not the post's.

The turn is the panel, and it is worth being precise about why the second half is not false modesty.

An institutional incentive is legible. Everyone knows a laboratory has reasons to look capable and a victim has reasons to look contained, and readers discount accordingly. The bias is priced in.

A thesis is not priced in, because nobody knows it is there unless the author says so. It shapes which material is gathered, which is emphasized, which is filed as complicating, and which never comes to mind at all — and it produces a book that reads as though the shape were found rather than brought.

And the response is an action rather than a sentence. The complicating facts go into the page plan, on the page, which is where the reader can see them.
