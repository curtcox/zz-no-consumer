# Page 042 — Panel 01

A public endpoint, labelled: customer-hosted CyberGym workload.

Around it, a larger boundary, labelled equally plainly: Modal platform — not compromised.

At the left margin, outside both, the board card from half past three, with a clock beside it.

The target is a customer's workload, not the platform around it.

The trust boundary is stated by the victim organization, by the investigators, and by Modal itself as quoted in the technical report.

This panel exists to prevent a specific error, and the book's notes forbid the error in the strongest terms it uses anywhere: never label this a platform breach or an escape.

Modal is a hosting company. A customer of Modal ran a CyberGym harness on it. That harness — the customer's own workload, running the customer's own code — was reachable from the public internet and was the thing that was exploited. Modal's isolation between customers was not defeated, and no account claims it was.

The distinction is not a technicality and it is not lawyerly caution. It is the difference between *a platform was broken into* and *something exposed on the internet was exploited*, and only the second is what happened.

The boundary in this frame stays intact for the whole page. The panel establishes it before anything else occurs.
