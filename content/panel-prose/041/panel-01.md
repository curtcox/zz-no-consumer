# Page 041 — Panel 01

The page splits vertically and stays split.

One hard rule from the top edge to the bottom. Two columns. Nothing crosses.

9 July 2026 — two things are happening.

The composition is editorial; the parallelism is the documented chronology.

Chapter 3 opens on a formal device rather than a scene, and the device is an argument. The reader is about to be shown two workstreams on one day, and the natural instinct — the instinct every account of this incident has followed, including an earlier draft of this book — is to join them. One thread finds a way in; the other looks for credentials; obviously the second was serving the first.

It was not. The times do not permit it, and the rest of the page will show why.

So the rule goes down the middle of the page and stays there, and no arrow crosses it anywhere, and the reader is invited to notice how badly they want one.

Two things are happening. That is the whole claim of the panel: not two parts of a thing, not a plan with two branches. Two things, in the same population, on the same board, on the same day.
