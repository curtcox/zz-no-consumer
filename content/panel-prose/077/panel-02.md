# Page 077 — Panel 02

Preprocessing. Chunks are joined into files. Messages are traced to writers and readers. Approximate action times are reconstructed.

Unmatched material stays visibly unmatched.

First, the record must be made queryable.

The investigators' reconstruction methods; the compression is the book's.

Nothing in the reservoirs arrives in a form anyone can ask questions of.

A board message was stored as many filesystem rows — page 075 established the ratio, roughly seventeen entries per message — so the message has to be reassembled before it is a message. Who wrote it and who read it are not fields; they are inferences from access patterns. Times are approximate because the record does not carry a clean clock.

All of that is reconstruction, performed before any analysis begins, and every step of it involves decisions: which chunks belong together, which access counts as reading, how to estimate a time.

And the unmatched material is left visible, which is the panel's most important detail. Fragments that could not be joined, messages whose writer could not be traced. They stay on the page as leftovers rather than being quietly dropped.

The queryable record is already a construction.
