# Page 077 — Panel 05

A published claim appears at the end of the chain, tagged with source, method, sample, and limitation.

The raw event remains several steps upstream.

Every conclusion passes through mediation.

A synthesis of the disclosed method.

Count the distance. Between an event in July and a sentence in a report there are: collection with gaps, reconstruction into queryable form, model classification, model ranking, model drafting, human spot-check, prompt revision, and expanded requests.

Eight steps, minimum, each of which could have gone differently.

The book is not saying the conclusions are wrong. It is saying they are conclusions — the output of a process, with the process's properties in them, rather than facts recovered whole from the world.

And the four tags on the published claim are what a reader needs in order to hold it correctly: what it came from, how it was produced, how much of the corpus it rests on, and what its limits are.

This is the same list Curt clipped to five evidence cards on page 074, arriving here as the investigators' own practice.

The raw event is upstream, and nobody ever sees it directly. Not the investigators, not the laboratory, not this book.
