# Page 077 — Panel 01

Two source reservoirs at the top of the page. Board dump. Transcripts.

Each carries gaps, timestamps, and selection notes — drawn on the reservoirs themselves rather than added afterward.

Records enter the pipeline without being labeled as conclusions.

The source types are factual; the diagram is editorial.

This is the chapter's clearest process diagram and it starts with a distinction most accounts skip: the difference between a record and a finding.

What is in these reservoirs is not knowledge. It is material — rows, tokens, timestamps, artifacts — which does not say anything until somebody asks it something. A twenty-million-entry dump has no opinion about what happened.

And the defects are drawn into the sources rather than mentioned later, because they are properties of the material rather than caveats about the conclusions. The gaps were there before anybody looked. The selection notes describe what was collected and what was not.

Everything published about this incident came out of these two reservoirs, through four more steps.

The page's whole purpose is that no published conclusion should appear to emerge directly from raw events.
