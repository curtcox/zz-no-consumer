# Page 010 — Panel 03

The panel splits, and a band of dusty amber runs down the middle of it.

On the left: what the board believed. The transcript-inspection diagram, the aperture, the line traced backwards from flag to path, all of it assembled over the previous two panels.

On the right: what the laboratory says actually ran. An answer arriving, an answer being accepted, and — conspicuously — no arrow reaching back into the transcript at all.

The investigators report that the population expected transcript review. The laboratory says its deployed scorer did not perform that check.

Both of those are documented statements by the parties who made them, and the book sets them side by side and does not adjudicate. It cannot. The exact evaluation prompt was never published. The scorer configuration was never published. The harness was never published. There is no third document to appeal to, and the two accounts that exist were written by organizations with different vantage points and different interests, neither of which is available for cross-examination in these pages.

So the amber gutter stays. It is not a dramatic device; it is a refusal, and it is the book's most frequently used one. The reader is being handed a conflict rather than a resolution, and being told which of the two claims each half of the page is standing on.

Everything the population does for the next four days rests on the left half.
