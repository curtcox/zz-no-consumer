# Page 044 — Panel 02

A parallel dossier view. OpenAI event table — 09:41 UTC.

Fourteen smaller sealed cards, beneath a heading: valid write-access tokens.

OpenAI: fourteen valid write-access tokens.

From the technical report.

One minute apart, and a factor of seven.

The panel is composed so the reader sees the arithmetic without being told: two large cards on the previous frame, fourteen small ones here, the same moment, the same event, the same population, two organizations.

And *write-access* is doing something the previous panel's *working* did not. It specifies what the credentials could do. A read token lets you look at what is public anyway; a write token lets you put things into repositories that other people trust. That distinction is the whole of what the following pages are about — the crafted dataset on page 045 requires exactly this and nothing else.

Fourteen of them. Valid.

Neither number is wrong. That is the difficulty, and the next panel is about how two careful organizations arrive one minute apart at counts that do not divide.
