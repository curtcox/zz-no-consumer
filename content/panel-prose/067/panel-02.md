# Page 067 — Panel 02

A large central dossier field, and the next six page numbers appear beyond the line in a visibly different register.

No METR mark crosses over. None.

Three cards stack beside the laboratory's published event table, and the exact times on it remain visible only as claims attributed to the reporting organization.

Underlying later-run records — not reviewed by METR. METR corroboration — not available. Primary account — OpenAI technical report.

One account. To the minute.

METR's scope limitation is documented; the slate and the evidentiary distinction are editorial.

That caption is the page's best sentence and it contains the whole difficulty.

The account that follows is extraordinarily precise. Times to the minute. Counts to the unit. Sequences of events laid out with the specificity of a forensic table, because it is one.

And precision is not corroboration. A single source can be exact and still be a single source, and the human instinct — reasonably, most of the time — is to read specificity as reliability.

Six pages of very specific claims are coming, from one organization, about itself, and nobody else has seen the material.
