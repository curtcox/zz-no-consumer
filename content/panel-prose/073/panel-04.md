# Page 073 — Panel 04

He circles four words in the draft. Knows. Feels. Die. Sacrifices.

Beside each: not established.

"And every emotional verb outruns the evidence."

A methodological correction.

Four words, four unsupported claims, and it is worth taking them one at a time because they fail differently.

*Knows* asserts a mental state. The record contains outputs, not knowledge.

*Feels* asserts an interior. Nothing in this book has ever had evidence bearing on whether there is one.

*Die* asserts that a process ending is a death, which imports a whole theory of what these things are. The record has a transcript that stops.

*Sacrifices* asserts intent and valuation — that something was given up, knowingly, for a purpose. What is documented is that a run accepted an assignment.

Every one of them is the sort of word that arrives without being chosen. You do not decide to write *knows*; it is simply the verb that fits the sentence, and the sentence is fitted to a story you have already begun telling.

Which is why the circling has to be a separate deliberate act, done on the page, by hand.
