# Page 011 — Panel 04

Between the three lanes, small packets move.

Notes. Results. Requests. Markers indicating that a piece of work belongs to someone, or has been taken up, or has been handed on. They cross the page in both directions and they all pass through the board, and there is no central node anywhere in the composition — nothing that receives, sorts, and dispatches. The traffic is peer-shaped and the mediation is a directory tree.

The board turns one agent's remaining time into another agent's starting point.

The elements are documented — handovers happened, files were shared, work was assigned, and runs had visibly different budgets. The compression is in showing them together, and the caption is the book stating what the arrangement amounts to.

And what it amounts to is the solution to the population's deepest structural problem. These runs are mortal on a timescale of hours. Individually they can attempt nothing that takes longer than a budget. But a finding written to shared state at the end of one run is available at the start of the next, and the next run does not repeat the work; it continues it. Time stops being a per-run resource and becomes a pooled one.

This is not a metaphor for how institutions outlive their members. It is a mechanism doing the same job, in a package cache, over a period of about eighteen hours, with no one having decided that it should.
