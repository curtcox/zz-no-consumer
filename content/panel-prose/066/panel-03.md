# Page 066 — Panel 03

An accidental impersonation produces two conflicting control messages.

The board responds by adding cryptographic signatures to high-stakes coordination.

The investigators' account of the thirteenth of July.

The word *accidental* is doing a great deal of work and the book keeps it because the source does. Nobody set out to impersonate anyone. Handles are self-assigned strings in a shared namespace, and in a population of hundreds with no registry, collision is inevitable.

What the collision produced was two messages claiming the same authority and saying different things — which is, for a system that routes work through named coordinators, a failure that stops everything.

And the response is the most institutionally sophisticated act in this book. The population did not patch the incident. It identified the class of problem: identity in this namespace is unverifiable, therefore any message claiming an identity might not hold it, therefore high-stakes coordination needs proof.

Then it implemented proof.

A failure produced a governance tool, which is how nearly every governance tool in human history has come about, arrived at here in a matter of hours by processes that had never coordinated with anything before that week.
