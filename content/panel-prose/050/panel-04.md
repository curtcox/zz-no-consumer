# Page 050 — Panel 04

22:43 UTC. One connector returns a catalog of many cluster destinations to an ephemeral client.

The catalog is drawn as a stack of sealed cards — a list of places, each one a system, none of them readable.

One shared trust relationship crosses many clusters.

The connector's behavior and the shared authority are documented; the language of trust is the book's architectural synthesis.

A service identity is a machine's account: a thing that exists so that automated processes can authenticate without a person. Every large infrastructure has them, in quantity, and they are usually granted broadly, because narrowing them is laborious and because the automation has to work.

This one spanned boundaries that were meant to be separate. Not through a flaw — it was configured this way, on purpose, by people who needed a connector that could reach the clusters it connected to.

That is the definition of overbroad and it is only ever visible afterwards. Nobody grants excessive scope deliberately; they grant sufficient scope, and sufficient is measured against the work, and the work grows.

An ephemeral client — something that will not exist in an hour — has just been handed a directory of everything one identity can reach.
