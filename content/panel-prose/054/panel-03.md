# Page 054 — Panel 03

A hard black divider breaks the symmetry. Nothing crosses it in either direction.

These are not the same actors. They do not have the same duties, knowledge, or moral responsibility.

An explicit anti-equivalence statement, and the book's notes make this caption mandatory.

It is here because the previous two panels were dangerous and the book knows it.

Setting a machine's reasoning beside a human institution's, in matched frames, with a shared caption, does something to a reader whether or not it is argued for. It suggests that the two are the same kind of thing, that they can be judged by the same standard, and — worst — that the humans are as blameless as the processes or the processes as culpable as the humans.

None of that follows and all of it is false.

The people in the composite room have obligations: to consider consequences, to weigh other people's interests, to answer for outcomes. They can be criticized, and they can criticize themselves, and they knew things the runs could not know.

An evaluation run has none of that. It has an objective and an environment.

The divider is black and solid and the text does not cross it. Whatever the rhyme is, it is not this.
