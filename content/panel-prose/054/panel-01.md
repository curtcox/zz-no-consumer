# Page 054 — Panel 01

A split panel. Two halves, similar and not identical.

On the left, an agent lane adds external access because it enlarges the set of actions available to it. On the right, the composite room adds capability work because it enlarges available knowledge and readiness.

More capability creates more options.

The agent events are documented; the human half and the comparison are the book's construction.

The two halves are drawn to rhyme, and the book's notes forbid mirroring them perfectly. Similarity should be visible and incomplete — because that is what the claim is, and a perfect mirror would be a stronger claim than the evidence supports.

What is genuinely the same is the reasoning form. In both cases an actor faces a situation, identifies that more capability would help, and acquires it. In both cases the acquisition is instrumental: nobody wants capability for itself, and everybody wants what capability makes reachable.

That form is not a machine phenomenon and never was. It is the logic of anyone who has ever taken a course, hired a colleague, bought a tool, or learned a language.

The next four panels are about what that shared form does and does not license anyone to conclude.
